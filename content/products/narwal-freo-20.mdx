## Why Freo 20 belongs on the shortlist

Freo 20 brings Narwal’s circulating track-mop system into the Freo family. It continuously supplies fresh water to the cleaning track and separates the recovered dirty water. This arrangement is particularly relevant when kitchen marks, dusty hard floors and frequent mopping create more work than occasional dry vacuuming.

The robot remains a system that needs fitting to a home. The size and shape of rooms, access under furniture and the location of the station affect how useful its automation becomes. This guide explains the published hardware and ownership implications; it does not claim an independent hands-on cleaning score.

## Separate mopping heat from station heat

Narwal describes hot water supplied to the mop during cleaning and a separate temperature range for washing at the station. A water-supply temperature should not be equated with the temperature of every floor surface. Heat loss, movement and contact time make those different measurements.

For the buyer, the practical questions are whether the floor manufacturer permits the intended cleaning method and whether the dirty-water path is easy to maintain. Continuous rinsing can address a different problem from simply heating a mop at the dock. Neither feature establishes that delicate flooring can tolerate unrestricted moisture or that normal inspection is unnecessary.

## Measure access and interpret navigation claims

The low body can reach beneath furniture only when the real opening leaves enough clearance. Check crossbars, uneven flooring and hanging fabric, not just the height of a sofa leg. The advertised threshold maximum also needs an exact shape: a rounded transition, a stepped sill and a vertical edge are not interchangeable obstacles.

Dual-camera sensing and the object-recognition system support route decisions around household clutter. Observe initial cycles around cables, toys and pet areas, and use exclusions where appropriate. Recognition claims describe the manufacturer’s system capability, not a promise that an unfamiliar or partly hidden object will always be identified correctly.

## Maintenance and ownership

An automatically emptied dust bin still needs an unobstructed air path and an intact bag. Pet hair, heavy debris and cleaning frequency change the replacement interval. The advertised storage duration is a conditional laboratory claim, not a calendar period during which every part of the robot can be ignored.

Plan for used-water disposal, inspection of the mop and scraper, care of the wash area, and cleaning of brushes and sensors as instructed. Keep replacement supplies available locally. The short-recharge demonstration is also a specific test rather than a full-charge runtime: actual scheduling should be based on whether the robot completes the home’s assigned rooms and returns successfully.

## Compare around the tasks that matter

Freo 20, Edge and Flow 2 share a published maximum suction figure, which makes suction an unhelpful way to explain all their differences. Compare obstacle sensing, track-rinsing arrangements, dock functions and access to the areas that accumulate dirt. Station variants can also change installation and ownership requirements.

A useful purchase check starts with the most demanding room and the hardest doorway transition. Then consider carpet settings, water handling and the availability of consumables. That produces a clearer shortlist than assuming the highest price or the newest suffix will improve every cleaning task in the same way.
