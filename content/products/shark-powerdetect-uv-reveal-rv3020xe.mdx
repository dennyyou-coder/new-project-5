## What UV Reveal is designed to do

PowerDetect UV Reveal adds UV illumination to a robot’s stain-detection approach. The [RV3020XE listing](https://www.sharkninja.com/shark-powerdetect-uv-reveal-2-in-1-vac-mop/RV3020XE.html) combines that sensing with a camera and a HyperSonic mop system. The useful question is whether it finds and treats recurring marks that a normal scheduled pass misses. A demonstration should use representative residue on the type of floor the owner actually has.

UV Reveal should not be described as disinfecting the floor. Detection and treatment are separate functions, and a station heating process is a third function. This guide does not convert any of those into a sterilization promise. WCB has not tested the robot; the account here is based on official material and a dated retail offer.

## Compare scrubbing mechanisms fairly

Shark’s 100-scrubs-per-minute figure describes its HyperSonic movement. A roller robot’s rpm describes a different motion, so placing the numbers side by side does not create a cleaning ranking. Inspect stain removal after drying, the number of repeat passes and the state of the mop after the job. Include an ordinary maintenance run as well as a conspicuous demonstration stain.

The seven-times scrubbing claim has a named Shark comparison model, RV2820ZE. It should remain attached to that comparison rather than becoming a statement about every competitor. Ask what operating conditions produced the claim and whether the same treatment is used in the planned schedule. Increased action on one patch may have different implications for time, moisture and care than routine room coverage.

## Route planning and the runtime discrepancy

The current official page gives two different endurance references: a higher bare-floor Eco maximum and a shorter dry-floor figure in its model table. This guide leaves the discrepancy visible. Neither should be used as a guaranteed duration for a combined vacuum-and-mop route. Obtain the supplied manual and assess the mode that will actually run in the home.

NeuroNav sensing and NeverStuck movement are relevant to obstacles, but a clear numerical crossing limit was not verified for this exact model. Test representative doorway tracks and furniture routes. Remove cords and hazardous messes rather than relying on a detection system to make every situation safe. For low furniture, obtain dimensions with an explicit robot-only scope before planning access.

## Maintenance and ownership

The station automates several tasks, but collected debris, moisture and cleaning parts still require attention. Follow the supplied instructions for the mop, brushes, filter and accessible passages. A washable pre-motor filter needs the specified drying time before reuse. After a difficult job, inspect the parts involved rather than relying exclusively on a long advertised interval.

Plan station placement around owner access as well as robot docking. Establish where wet parts will be cleaned and dried and where replacement consumables can be purchased locally. Keep any station sanitation claim tied to its stated process and conditions. It does not describe the cleanliness of every surface the robot has crossed or remove the need to handle an unsuitable spill separately.

## WCB buying view

UV Reveal is worth a demonstration where stain detection and sonic treatment address a recurring problem. Compare [C28](/products/eufy-omni-c28) and [T80S OMNI](/products/ecovacs-deebot-t80s-omni) for a different roller-based approach. Choose using route completion, dried-floor results and ongoing care. Resolve the official runtime inconsistency and exact package details before making those figures part of the purchase decision.
