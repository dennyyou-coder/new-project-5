**Samsung-branded home appliances are made and marketed through Samsung Electronics Co., Ltd. and its regional operating network.** There is no separate universal manufacturer called “Samsung Appliances.” Samsung Electronics runs the Digital Appliances business, develops the product and software platforms, and uses its own subsidiaries, local factories and product-specific supply arrangements to serve different markets.

That does not mean every Samsung refrigerator, washer, oven or vacuum comes from South Korea—or even from a Samsung-owned factory. The correct manufacturer, country of origin, importer and warranty provider can change by product, model and destination market.

## What Company Actually Runs Samsung Home Appliances?

The company-level answer is **Samsung Electronics Co., Ltd.** Samsung Electronics is the listed technology company that reports the appliance business inside its Device eXperience, or DX, organization. Its corporate reporting identifies Digital Appliances alongside other consumer businesses rather than as a stand-alone appliance company.

This distinction matters because the word “Samsung” is also used across a much wider Korean business group. A Samsung-branded refrigerator is not made by every company carrying the Samsung name. The relevant chain normally starts with Samsung Electronics, then moves to a regional sales or manufacturing subsidiary and finally to the exact product label and invoice.

In the United States, for example, Samsung Electronics America supports the market while Samsung Electronics Home Appliances America operates the Newberry manufacturing facility. Other countries use different local entities. The company named on the warranty, conformity declaration or importer label may therefore differ from the Korean parent.

## Where Are Samsung Appliances Made?

Samsung uses a global production network. Public corporate reports describe international subsidiaries and manufacturing sites across major regions, while local announcements document plants built to serve nearby demand.

The clearest U.S. example is **Newberry, South Carolina**. Samsung announced the facility in 2017 and opened it for residential washing-machine production. At its fifth anniversary, Samsung said the site had produced more than four million washing machines and was its first U.S.-based home-appliance manufacturing facility.

That is strong evidence for designated washing machines. It is not evidence that every Samsung appliance sold in the United States is made in South Carolina. Refrigerators, cooking products, dishwashers, vacuums and different washer generations can follow other production routes.

The same limitation applies globally. A company may localize assembly, source a component from another country or use an outside supplier for a particular accessory while retaining design, quality and brand control. Buyers should treat country of origin as a model-level fact, not a permanent brand attribute.

## Does Samsung Manufacture Every Product It Sells?

Samsung Electronics is a large manufacturer, but a catalogue-wide “made entirely by Samsung” claim is still too broad.

Modern appliance production combines final assembly, motors, compressors, electronics, batteries, displays, software and packaging. Some elements can come from Samsung affiliates or external suppliers. Accessories and replacement parts can also use different production arrangements from the finished appliance.

For a precise answer, collect the model code, serial range, rating plate, carton origin, manual, responsible-company statement and conformity document. Those records are more reliable than a marketplace listing or a factory name attached to a different model.

## What Is Samsung Bespoke AI?

**Bespoke AI** is Samsung's current umbrella for connected and AI-enabled home appliances. Samsung presents it across refrigeration, laundry, cooking, dishwashing and floor care, with SmartThings providing much of the cross-device connection.

The proposition can include sensors, cameras, screens, automated cycle selection, energy-management functions and software services. Samsung's 2025 AI Home material also connected household routines across appliances and showed Jet Bot cleaning products responding within the broader smart-home system.

Bespoke AI should not be treated as one technical specification. A refrigerator with a display, a washing machine with AI-assisted cycle selection and a robot vacuum with object recognition perform different tasks. Feature availability can depend on model, software version, language, account, Wi-Fi connection and region.

For commercial buyers, the software layer creates additional checks:

1. Which functions work without a cloud account?
2. Which entity provides SmartThings in the destination country?
3. How long are security and software updates promised for the model?
4. Can the product operate if a service is unavailable?
5. What data, camera or microphone controls apply to the installation?

## Does Samsung Make Vacuum Cleaners and Floor-Care Products?

Yes. Samsung's cleaning range includes **Jet cordless vacuums** and **Jet Bot robotic vacuums**, with availability varying by country.

Samsung launched the Bespoke AI Jet Lite as a lighter cordless model and has used AI Cleaning Mode across parts of the Jet range. Its AI Home demonstrations have also placed robot vacuums inside SmartThings routines. At IFA 2025, Samsung highlighted the Bespoke AI Jet Bot Steam Ultra with upgraded object-recognition functions.

This makes Samsung directly relevant to cleaning-appliance buyers, not merely adjacent through refrigerators and washing machines. Retailers that already buy Samsung major appliances may evaluate floor care as part of the same brand, connected-home and service proposition.

The shared name still does not prove shared manufacturing. A refrigerator plant, washer factory and vacuum production line can sit in different countries and use different suppliers. Battery packs, dust bags, filters, brushes and docking stations also need their own continuity and compliance checks.

## Are Samsung, SmartThings and Bespoke the Same Company?

No. They describe different layers of the offer.

- **Samsung Electronics** is the company operating the electronics and appliance businesses.
- **Samsung** is the customer-facing master brand used across multiple product categories.
- **Digital Appliances** is the relevant operating business inside Samsung Electronics.
- **Bespoke** is a product and design proposition used on selected appliances.
- **Bespoke AI** describes AI-enabled products and experiences within that proposition.
- **SmartThings** is Samsung's connected-device platform and service ecosystem.

These layers can appear together on one product, but they do not replace the legal manufacturer, importer or warranty provider printed in local documents.

## How Does Samsung Appliance Warranty Work?

Warranty coverage is regional and product-specific. Samsung's current U.S. appliance warranty page says most home appliances include a one-year manufacturer's warranty for parts and labor, while selected components and promotional programs can carry different terms.

That U.S. statement must not be copied into another country. The warranty provider, registration requirement, labor coverage, commercial-use exclusion and service route can change by market and product category.

Before purchase, compare the sales invoice with the warranty document and confirm that the model was intended for the destination country. Grey-market imports can create problems with parts, connected services, voltage, certification and authorized repair.

| Country-of-origin marking | Stated origin for that unit, not the brand generally |

| Importer label | Entity responsible for placing it on the destination market |

For suppliers, a Samsung group name or procurement contact is not enough. Confirm which Samsung Electronics entity or authorized partner is issuing the request, which plant or program it covers, and whether the commercial documents match the product and territory.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who makes Samsung appliances?

Samsung Electronics runs the Digital Appliances business and makes products through a global network of subsidiaries, factories and product-specific supply arrangements.

### Are Samsung appliances made in South Korea?

Some Samsung appliance operations and production are based in Korea, but the manufacturing network is global. Check the rating plate and documents for the exact model.

### Are Samsung washing machines made in the USA?

Samsung's Newberry, South Carolina facility produces residential washing machines for the U.S. market. That does not mean every Samsung washer sold in America comes from that plant.

### Does Samsung make its own refrigerators?

Samsung Electronics operates and manufactures the refrigerator business, but the factory and component supply chain vary by model and market.

### Who makes Samsung vacuum cleaners?

Samsung Electronics operates the Jet and Jet Bot cleaning ranges. The exact production site and responsible regional entity should be verified from model documents.

### Is Bespoke a separate appliance company?

No. Bespoke is a Samsung product proposition. Bespoke AI adds connected and AI-enabled features to selected Samsung appliances.

### Is SmartThings required for every Samsung appliance?

No. Many core appliance functions work locally, while connected or AI features may require SmartThings, an account and network access. Check the exact model.

## Final Answer

Samsung appliances are made and marketed by Samsung Electronics through its Digital Appliances business and a global regional network. Samsung operates major manufacturing facilities—including verified U.S. washing-machine production in Newberry, South Carolina—but no single factory makes the entire appliance range.

Bespoke AI and SmartThings connect refrigerators, laundry, cooking and cleaning products under one experience. For buyers, the essential work remains model-specific: identify the local seller, manufacturer or responsible entity, factory and country of origin, importer, software terms and warranty provider before treating “Samsung” as a complete supply-chain answer.
