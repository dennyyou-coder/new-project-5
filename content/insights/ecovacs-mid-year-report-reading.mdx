A few days ago, while I was hospitalized with chickenpox, Ecovacs released its mid-year annual report. I have briefly excerpted some key points for your reference.

Firstly, the overall performance declined by 3.8% compared to the same period last year. Among them, the ODM for service robots decreased by 84.47%, while the OEM/ODM for cleaning small appliances dropped by 13.87%. This signals a significant shift towards proprietary brands for Ecovacs. From a product perspective, robot vacuum ODM revenue has almost fallen to only RMB 38.65 million, with no noticeable impact from completely abandoning it. For cleaning small appliances, there is still an amount close to 600 million RMB (accounting for 24.7% of total performance) in OEM/ODM orders. If these were all abandoned, it might have a certain short-term impact on the listed company.

> 1. Strategic reduction of the service robot ODM business. To focus resources on growing Ecovacs-brand service robots worldwide, and to reduce the potential impact of ODM work on its own brand in each market and channel, the company decided to scale back and gradually stop service robot ODM. As a result, related revenue fell 84.47% year on year. Because ODM margins were high, net profit also fell compared with the same period last year. (Ecovacs 2019 interim report, translated)

Especially at this juncture of the Sino-US trade war, how to make choices may be a question that Ecovacs CEO Mr. Qian needs to consider.

> 5. Building the Tineco brand of smart cleaning appliances. The company continued last year's push to build its own smart cleaning appliance brand, investing at home and abroad to raise Tineco's profile and sales. Tineco sales on Amazon US kept growing fast and began to enter offline channels; offline channels in China are being built out. 2019 will be Tineco's main investment period, and Tineco's share of the cleaning appliance business is expected to keep rising. (Ecovacs 2019 interim report, translated)

Ecovacs has developed the Tineco brand for intelligent small household appliances focused on cleaning. During this period, advertisements for the Tineco smart vacuum cleaner can be seen in many places. It is evident that Ecovacs places high hopes on the Tineco brand.

> Tineco: from functional to smart. The smart vacuum that thinks. (Tineco advertisement, translated)

However, the pricing of Tineco's vacuum cleaners is particularly puzzling, as they are priced similarly to Dyson models. There is a well-known industry rule that those priced close to Dyson will perish. A search for "Tineco vacuum cleaner" on Taobao reveals two-digit sales figures on the homepage, which validates the reasonableness of this pricing strategy.

In the vacuum cleaner industry, both listed companies Kingclean's Mojie and Ecovacs' Tineco have demonstrated through actual sales that pricing close to Dyson is a dead end.

From my perspective, positioning Tineco or Mojie as the top domestic brand might be the most practical approach. Setting the sales price around 1,999 yuan would likely achieve certain sales volumes while still maintaining reasonable profit margins.

> Revenue: mainly because the company adjusted its sales mix. Own-brand revenue kept growing, while contract manufacturing revenue, especially service robot ODM, fell sharply. Cost of sales: mainly due to lower revenue. Selling expenses: mainly higher payroll, from hiring local staff abroad to grow the robot brand overseas and building a team for Tineco. Administrative expenses: mainly higher payroll and office rent. Finance costs: mainly higher exchange losses and cash discounts. R&D expenses: mainly higher payroll, from recruiting more senior R&D talent. Operating cash flow: mainly because the company used up inventory from the end of last period and kept stock under control, so it paid less for goods and services. Investing cash flow: mainly fewer purchases of wealth management products. Financing cash flow: mainly cash dividends paid this period, and IPO proceeds received in the prior period. (Ecovacs 2019 interim report, translated)
