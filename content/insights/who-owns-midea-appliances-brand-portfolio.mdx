**Midea appliances are operated by Midea Group Co., Ltd., a publicly traded Chinese technology and manufacturing company listed in Shenzhen and Hong Kong.** Midea is both the name of the listed group and its core consumer-appliance brand. It is not an appliance label owned by a larger foreign parent.

The group has expanded far beyond one brand. Its home-appliance portfolio includes Midea, Little Swan, COLMO, Comfee and other regional names, while acquisitions added specific rights and businesses connected to **Toshiba Lifestyle, Eureka and Teka**. These relationships are not identical: one involves majority ownership plus a long-term brand license, another was a vacuum-brand asset acquisition, and the newest was a group acquisition with an explicit territorial exclusion.

## What Company Actually Owns the Midea Brand?

The company-level answer is **Midea Group Co., Ltd.** The business began in Shunde, China, in 1968 and developed from fan production into a global appliance and technology group. It listed in Shenzhen in 2013 and completed a Hong Kong listing in September 2024.

Because the company is publicly traded, “who owns Midea” does not have one simple parent-company name. Shareholders own the listed company through the two markets, while its board and management govern the group. Founder He Xiangjian remains central to the company's history, but a founder biography should not replace current shareholder and governance records.

Within the group, consumer appliances sit primarily in the **Smart Home Business**. Midea's corporate descriptions also identify Industrial Technologies, Building Technologies, Robotics & Automation, Midea Medical and Annto Logistics as other major businesses. A refrigerator supplier and an industrial-robot supplier can therefore both work with Midea Group while dealing with different organizations, companies and procurement systems.

For an appliance transaction, the practical chain is usually:

1. Midea Group provides the listed-company and strategic platform.
2. Smart Home Business manages the consumer-appliance portfolio.
3. A brand, product division or specialized subsidiary controls the program.
4. A regional company or distributor places the product on the market.
5. A designated factory makes the exact model.

## Which Home-Appliance Brands Belong to Midea?

Midea presents a broad brand matrix. Its own group materials name full-category or major consumer brands such as **Midea, Toshiba and Comfee**, alongside more specialized names including **Little Swan, COLMO, Eureka and Teka**. Other brands serve industrial or business-to-business markets rather than ordinary household appliances.

The portfolio gives Midea coverage across air treatment, refrigeration, laundry, cooking, dishwashing, water appliances, small kitchen appliances and floor care. It also creates an important research problem: the same parent does not give every brand the same trademark rights, company structure, factories or distribution territory.

Three acquisitions show why the exact transaction matters.

## What Part of Toshiba Appliances Does Midea Own?

In 2016, Midea acquired **80.1% of Toshiba Lifestyle Products & Services Corporation**, the home-appliance business commonly referred to as Toshiba Lifestyle. Midea's annual report also described a **40-year global license** to use the Toshiba brand for the covered home-appliance business.

This is a majority-owned operating company plus a trademark license—not ownership of the entire Toshiba corporate group. Toshiba-branded infrastructure, elevators, electronic components and other businesses can follow different corporate paths. Even within appliances, local product documents and trademark notices determine which company and license apply.

For buyers, “Toshiba is owned by Midea” is therefore too broad. A more precise statement is that Midea holds the controlling interest in Toshiba Lifestyle and long-term brand rights for the covered appliance business. The exact seller, importer and warranty provider can still differ by country.

## How Is Eureka Connected to Midea?

Midea acquired the **North American Eureka vacuum-cleaner brand and most related assets** from Electrolux in December 2016. Electrolux's annual reporting disclosed gross consideration of about US$40 million; a widely repeated US$60 million number referred to prior sales, not the purchase price.

Eureka now gives Midea a separate cleaning-appliance identity across upright, stick, canister, wet-dry and robotic products. Midea America operates the U.S. Eureka website, while model documents and regulatory records identify specialized companies such as Midea Robozone for selected robot-vacuum platforms.

The boundary matters. **Sanitaire** was sold separately by Electrolux to BISSELL in 2018, and **Eureka Forbes** is a different Indian company. Neither should be placed inside Midea's Eureka organization merely because of historical or naming overlap.

World Clean Biz's detailed [Eureka ownership guide](/blog/who-owns-eureka-midea-electrolux-manufacturing) explains the transaction price, legal entities, robot-platform evidence and excluded brands.

## What Did Midea Acquire From Teka?

Midea completed the acquisition of **Teka Group** on April 29, 2025 after announcing the proposed transaction in 2024. The closing release explicitly excluded **Teka Rus LLC**.

The transaction added a European- and Latin American-centered kitchen and home-solutions business with the **Teka, Kuppersbusch and Intra** brands. Teka's product base includes ovens, cooktops, hoods, sinks and faucets, and the group brought its own factories, commercial network and brand heritage.

The exclusion should remain visible in ownership research. A group announcement cannot be copied into a Russian-market claim without checking the local entity and trademark position. The same principle applies when a distributor seeks authorization for only one Teka Group brand, product class or territory.

## Where Are Midea Appliances Manufactured?

Midea operates one of the appliance industry's largest global production systems. Its current investor page, citing the 2025 annual report, says the localized self-operated business covers 50 major countries and identifies **43 major manufacturing bases overseas** plus 29 overseas research and development centers.

Other Midea announcements use different totals because they were published on different dates or count global, overseas, acquired and specialized facilities differently. For example, the April 2025 Teka completion release described 44 manufacturing bases and separately noted Teka's ten factories. Later corporate updates can include additional capacity. These numbers should be dated and defined rather than combined into one artificial total.

At the product level, Midea uses a mix of core Midea plants, specialized subsidiaries, acquired-company factories and approved supply partners. A Midea refrigerator, Toshiba washer, Teka oven and Eureka robot vacuum can all sit within the group while coming from different factories and technical organizations.

## Are Midea Appliances Made in China?

Many Midea appliances and components are made in China, where the company has its headquarters and extensive industrial base. But “Midea appliances are made in China” is not a reliable global answer.

Midea has expanded local manufacturing to serve overseas markets, and acquired companies brought additional plants and product specializations. The country of origin can change with model, production period and destination. A marketplace listing or parent-company address is not customs evidence.

Use the exact rating label, carton, declaration, importer record and supplier statement. If origin affects tariff treatment, public procurement or marketing, obtain a declaration tied to the SKU and shipment—not a group brochure.

## Does Midea Manufacture Every Appliance in Its Portfolio?

Midea is a large direct manufacturer, but ownership does not prove that every product and component is made in a Midea-owned facility.

The group's scale supports internal design, tooling, motors, compressors, controls and assembly across many categories. Acquired businesses can retain their own production systems, and approved suppliers can manufacture selected finished products, accessories or components. Production assignments also change as new factories open and platforms are localized.

For B2B qualification, ask who controls the design, who owns the tooling, which legal entity signs the quality agreement, which factory is approved, whether subcontracting is permitted and how manufacturing changes must be notified. Those answers matter more than a brand-level claim of complete vertical integration.

## How Does Midea Connect Large Appliances, Small Appliances and Cleaning?

Midea is particularly relevant to cleaning-industry suppliers because it spans all three buying contexts.

- **Large appliances:** laundry and dishwashing create demand for motors, pumps, valves, plastics, electronics and service parts.
- **Small appliances:** kitchen, air-care and water products share retail buyers, component categories and manufacturing capabilities with floor care.
- **Cleaning appliances:** Midea-branded vacuums, Midea Robozone platforms and Eureka products cover cordless, robotic and wet-dry formats in different markets.

This breadth can create cross-category commercial opportunities, but it does not mean one sourcing manager buys for every brand. A component approved for a Midea-branded floor washer may need separate validation for Eureka. A retailer agreement for Toshiba Lifestyle does not automatically include Teka or Midea products.

## What Buyers and Suppliers Should Verify

| Document or check | What it should establish |
|---|---|
| Listed-company and business unit | Whether the opportunity belongs to Midea Group, Smart Home or another business |
| Contracting legal entity | Company signing the order, quality agreement and payment instructions |
| Brand and territory | Midea, Toshiba Lifestyle, Eureka, Teka or another brand, with geographic scope |
| Model and serial data | Exact SKU, electrical rating, software variant and production traceability |
| Factory evidence | Approved production site and whether it is group-owned, acquired or contracted |
| Country-of-origin record | Origin for the actual shipment rather than the parent-company address |
| Trademark notice | Owned or licensed rights, product class, duration and regional exclusions |
| Compliance file | Responsible signatory, importer, standards and covered model list |
| Warranty and service | Local provider, claims process, parts, software support and recall responsibility |
| Change-control terms | Approval process for factory, supplier, component or specification changes |

When validating a supplier contact, compare the email domain, local company registration, physical address and bank account. A brand portfolio is not permission to substitute one Midea-related legal entity for another.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who owns Midea appliances?

Midea Group Co., Ltd. operates Midea appliances. It is a publicly traded company listed in Shenzhen and Hong Kong rather than a brand owned by another appliance conglomerate.

### Is Midea a Chinese company?

Yes. Midea was founded in Shunde, China, and is headquartered in Guangdong. It now operates a large international research, manufacturing and sales network.

### Does Midea own Toshiba appliances?

Midea acquired 80.1% of Toshiba Lifestyle Products & Services and a 40-year Toshiba brand license for the covered home-appliance business in 2016. It does not own every Toshiba business.

### Does Midea own Eureka?

Yes. Midea acquired the North American Eureka vacuum business and most related assets from Electrolux in 2016. Sanitaire and Eureka Forbes are separate.

### Does Midea own Teka?

Yes, with an important boundary. Midea completed the Teka Group acquisition in April 2025, excluding Teka Rus LLC.

### Where are Midea appliances made?

Midea uses factories in China and a large overseas manufacturing network. The exact country and factory must be checked for the specific Midea, Toshiba, Eureka or Teka model.

### Are Midea and Eureka the same brand?

No. Eureka belongs to Midea Group but remains a separate cleaning-appliance brand with its own market presentation and regional operating routes.

### Does one Midea warranty cover every portfolio brand?

No. Warranty providers and terms are brand-, product- and country-specific. Use the local terms for the exact model.

## Final Answer

Midea appliances are operated by Midea Group Co., Ltd., a publicly traded Chinese group listed in Shenzhen and Hong Kong. Its Smart Home Business supports a broad consumer portfolio spanning major appliances, small appliances and floor care.

The Toshiba, Eureka and Teka relationships have different legal boundaries. Midea acquired 80.1% of Toshiba Lifestyle and long-term Toshiba appliance brand rights in 2016; acquired Eureka's North American vacuum business from Electrolux in 2016; and completed the Teka Group acquisition in 2025 while excluding Teka Rus. Midea's large international factory network provides scale, but it does not identify the origin or responsible company for every product. Buyers and suppliers should verify the exact brand, model, legal seller, factory, importer, trademark scope and warranty provider.
