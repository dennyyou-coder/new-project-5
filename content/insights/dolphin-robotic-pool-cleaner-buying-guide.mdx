**The best Dolphin robotic pool cleaner is not one universal model. It is the exact Dolphin SKU that matches your pool length, surface, debris and required cleaning zones—and comes with a warranty and repair route that works in your market.** Start by deciding whether the robot must clean only the floor, the floor and walls, or the floor, walls and waterline. Then choose between a corded platform and a Liberty cordless model, compare the filter system, and verify the product number with an authorized seller.

Dolphin is a Maytronics brand, but the range is broad. A compact Nautilus CC, connected S400, cordless Liberty 600, feature-rich M550 and commercial Wave 80 do not solve the same job. Model names, channel availability and warranty terms can also vary by country and seller. A useful comparison therefore begins with the pool—not a list of brand features.

## Start With Pool Fit

Record the pool before comparing Dolphin families:

- in-ground or above-ground construction;
- maximum length, width and depth;
- surface material, including plaster, tile, fiberglass or vinyl;
- flat, sloped, bowl-shaped or multi-level floor;
- main drains, ladders and permanent obstacles;
- steps, benches, sun shelves and safety ledges;
- the debris that dominates during the season.

The “up to 33 feet” or “up to 50 feet” statement is a first filter. It does not prove that the robot will handle every transition or obstacle. A smooth tiled wall, raised drain, shallow ledge or sharp floor-to-wall angle can change traction and coverage.

Check the pool classification on the exact model page as well. Some current Dolphin pages list both in-ground and above-ground use, while others are positioned around a particular residential or commercial application. The [above-ground versus in-ground pool robot guide](/blog/above-ground-vs-in-ground-robotic-pool-cleaners) explains why weight, geometry and wall-climbing expectations differ.

If the pool has an unusual layout, ask the seller for a written compatibility answer and the return or trial policy. A demonstration in a similar pool is more useful than a generic maximum-length claim.

## Decide the Cleaning Zones Before the Model

“Dolphin robotic pool cleaner” does not mean every Dolphin cleans the same areas.

### Floor and walls

Nautilus CC, S100 and Liberty 200 are current examples whose official pages list floor and wall cleaning. They can be sensible shortlists when the buyer does not require an explicit waterline-cleaning claim.

### Floor, walls and waterline

S400, M550, Liberty 400, Liberty 600 and T3000 pages list waterline cleaning in addition to floor and walls. That difference is material. A robot that climbs the wall is not automatically a robot designed to travel and scrub along the waterline.

### Steps, benches and sun shelves

These are difficult surfaces for underwater robots. The current T3000 page explicitly lists steps and a sun ledge, but buyers should still test the actual geometry. A robot may reach a feature without cleaning its entire horizontal surface, and shallow ledges can interrupt traction or water flow.

Write the required zones into the purchase request. “Floor, wall and waterline” is more testable than “full pool cleaning.”

## Corded Dolphin vs Cordless Liberty

The corded-versus-cordless decision affects daily handling, cycle planning and long-term service.

### Corded platforms

A corded model avoids charging between cycles and battery aging, but the power supply needs a safe location and the cable needs storage. Swivel-cable designs can reduce cable management problems; they do not eliminate the need to inspect, rinse and coil the cable correctly.

Corded products such as S400, M550 and T3000 are reasonable candidates when the pool is used frequently, the power-supply position is practical and the owner values repeated cycles without battery charging. Confirm cable length against the farthest point of the pool rather than the nearest outlet.

### Cordless Liberty platforms

A Liberty model removes the floating cable during cleaning, but adds a battery, charger and retrieval workflow. Compare:

- listed pool length and runtime;
- floor/wall versus waterline coverage;
- charging location and environmental limits;
- retrieval method and wet lifting;
- replacement battery or authorized repair route;
- storage requirements during the off-season.

Liberty 200 and Liberty 400 illustrate why “cordless” is not one specification. Their listed pool length is similar, but the official pages describe different coverage and controls. Liberty 600 moves the cordless shortlist to pools listed up to 50 feet and adds a different feature package.

Do not choose cordless only because it appears simpler. Choose it when cable-free operation is worth the charging and battery-service obligations.

## Match the Filtration to the Debris

The filter must capture the pool’s real debris without becoming a constant restriction.

- Coarser debris includes leaves, seed pods and larger insects.
- Fine debris includes sand, silt and dust.
- Mixed pools may need a layered or interchangeable filter strategy.
- Hair and fibrous debris can wrap around brushes or intake areas even when the filter basket is not full.

Maytronics pages use terms such as top-load basket, multi-layer filtration, combined filter and ultra-fine filtration. Treat these as model-specific configurations. Ask what comes in the box and what is optional.

Finer filtration is not automatically better for every cycle. A fine screen can load quickly after a storm, reducing water flow and cleaning performance. A practical workflow may use a coarser pass first, followed by a fine-filtration pass.

Before buying, check how the filter is removed, opened and rinsed. For a dealer demonstration, have the customer handle a wet basket and reinstall it. Convenience claims are more credible when the buyer can complete the maintenance process without tools or confusion.

## Navigation, Apps and Cleaning Cycles

An app is useful for scheduling, status and mode selection, but it is not proof of better physical coverage. Pool geometry, traction, brush condition, water flow and filter loading still determine whether the robot reaches the required areas.

Compare the available cycles on the exact model. S400 and T3000 pages currently list multiple cycle durations, while S100 is positioned around a simpler two-hour cycle. The useful question is whether the chosen modes correspond to the pool’s workload.

For app-connected products, verify:

- which functions work locally and which require an account or internet connection;
- whether more than one family member or property manager can use the robot;
- update and reset procedures;
- what the robot can still do if the app or wireless connection is unavailable.

For hotels, apartments and managed communities, a consumer app may be less important than repeatable operating procedures, staff training and repair turnaround.

## Handling, Retrieval and Storage

Dry product weight does not describe the full handling task. The robot is heavier when lifted from water and may retain debris until it drains.

Check:

- whether the robot can be brought to the waterline predictably;
- the grip points and lift path;
- drainage speed;
- whether a caddy is included or optional;
- where the cable, power supply or charger will be stored;
- whether the operator can move the unit without pulling the cable.

Liberty products use cordless retrieval features that differ by model. Corded premium products may include a caddy. Confirm the box contents rather than assuming every product in a family includes the same accessories.

## Warranty, Parts and Dealer Support

Service is where two apparently similar Dolphin offers can become very different.

Maytronics provides a [store locator](https://www.maytronics.com/en-us/store-locator/) that describes dealer capabilities such as certified service, warranty processing, certified parts, product display and potential trial access. Its [support portal](https://www.maytronics.com/en-us/support.html) also provides warranty registration, manuals, repair requests and parts routes.

Before paying, record:

1. the exact model and product number;
2. the authorized seller and country of sale;
3. the written warranty term for that SKU;
4. who processes a warranty claim;
5. the nearest authorized repair location;
6. expected access to filters, brushes, tracks, cable, power supply, charger and battery service;
7. shipping responsibility if local service is unavailable.

Do not assume that a family name guarantees one warranty term. Channel-specific or regional models can have different documentation. Save the invoice, product page and warranty terms at purchase.

This is also why a lower online price may not be the lowest ownership cost. Dealer commissioning, compatibility advice, demonstrations, spare parts and faster repair can have real value—especially during a short swimming season.

## Dealer and Distributor Evaluation

A dealer assortment should be built around customer jobs, not the largest possible model count.

A practical range may include:

- a simpler 33-foot floor-and-wall role;
- a 50-foot corded floor/wall/waterline role;
- a cordless role with clearly defined battery and charger support;
- a higher-spec model for difficult zones or customers who value caddy, app and filter options;
- a separate commercial route rather than using residential units as substitutes.

For each stocked SKU, create a one-page matrix covering pool type, length, surface, cleaning zones, box contents, consumables, warranty process and repair destination. Train sales staff to quote the product number, not only the family name.

Dealers should also track first-cycle failures by pool geometry and debris type. That feedback is more useful than a generic return rate because it shows where qualification questions are weak.

### Is a cordless Dolphin better than a corded Dolphin?

## The Bottom Line

Choose a Dolphin robotic pool cleaner by working through five gates: pool fit, cleaning zones, corded or cordless power, filtration and service. The model name is useful only after those requirements are clear.

For a homeowner, the safest purchase is the exact SKU that has a written compatibility answer, a manageable maintenance routine and a local warranty route. For a dealer or distributor, the strongest assortment gives each model a defined customer job and makes parts and repair responsibility visible before the sale.

That approach is less exciting than declaring one “best Dolphin,” but it produces a better match—and a more defensible buying decision.
