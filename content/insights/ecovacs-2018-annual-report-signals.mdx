**Business overview: the Ecovacs and Tineco brands.**

> Ecovacs develops, designs, makes and sells home service robots, cleaning small appliances and related parts, and is one of the world's best-known home service robot makers. Its robot line covers floor-cleaning robots (DEEBOT), window-cleaning robots (WINBOT) and air-purifying robots (ATMOBOT). Its floor robots moved from random cleaning to semi-planned and fully planned navigation, and then to AIVI machine vision. The Ecovacs brand leads the Chinese market and has entered the US, Europe and Japan. In small appliances, Ecovacs provides OEM/ODM services to vacuum brands at home and abroad and sells its own TINECO brand, whose main products are cordless handheld vacuums. (Ecovacs 2018 annual report, translated)

**R&D team and industrial design.**

> Ecovacs has an R&D team of nearly 800 people covering sensors, structure, engineering, algorithms, internet, big data and IoT. In July 2018 it set up the Ecovacs (Nanjing) AI Research Institute. Awards in 2018 included a CES 2018 Innovation Award for WINBOT WA3 in January; "Best Buy" and "Best Choice" ratings from Consumer Reports and Forbes for DEEBOT models sold overseas; an iF design award for ATMOBOT AA3 in February; Red Dot awards for AA3 and WA3 in April; IDEA awards from the Industrial Designers Society of America in June; and a 2018 international CMF design award for DEEBOT DN5 in November. (Ecovacs 2018 annual report, translated)

The next direction for development is sensors. Tineco's latest cordless vacuum cleaners have already begun using a dust sensor to automatically adjust suction power.

> Besides upgrading its current product lines, Ecovacs is investing in next-generation sensor modules, machine vision, deep-learning efficiency and cloud–device computing, aiming at a future "butler" class of home robot. At the end of 2018 it had 788 R&D staff. The Nanjing AI institute, set up in July 2018, had a team of more than 10 people by year-end and had built AI compute nodes, GPU workstations, distributed storage and a mobile-robot test platform. (Ecovacs 2018 annual report, translated)

Revenue in 2018 had already reached RMB 5.694 billion, representing a growth of 25.11%. At this rate, Ecovacs is expected to surpass Kingclean and become the top brand in the vacuum cleaner industry in 2019.

The report gives two reasons. Ecovacs expected its US business to keep growing quickly in 2019, so it shipped stock to the US early because of the trade dispute and built channel stock in other overseas markets, which raised inventory. It also cut bought-in and sales of low-end service robots in 2018 to raise the share of planned-navigation models, especially fully planned ones.

**Operating expenses.**

Selling expenses rose 47.39% to RMB 1,071.5 million as Ecovacs-brand robot sales grew, overseas markets expanded and Tineco spent to build its market. Administrative expenses rose 4.76% to RMB 293.6 million. R&D expenses rose 65.34% to RMB 205.1 million, mainly for new sensor modules and machine vision and higher R&D pay. Finance expenses swung to a net gain of RMB 27.8 million, mainly from exchange gains.

**Ownership and control.**

Ecovacs is controlled by founder Qian Dongqi (钱东奇) and his son David Cheng Qian. David Cheng Qian owns 100% of Ever Group (13.05%) and Sky Sure (2.25%).

Airuite: makes road sweepers and floor scrubbers for municipal cleaning. Autonomous sweepers and scrubbers look like a direction Ecovacs may also move into.

Lepaite: programmable robot toys and coding kits for children. Smart toys for the family are another possible direction for Ecovacs.

Xianzhi Technology (Seer Robotics): laser-navigation mobile robots. Its website shows products aimed at commercial and industrial use, such as AGVs and autonomous forklifts.

Gouweicao Technology (Gowild): AI companions such as a holographic smart speaker and the Gongzi Xiaobai home robot. Will future robot vacuums carry a virtual butler?

Zhujian Technology (Emotibot): multimodal emotional AI that reads text, images and voice. This is likely meant to strengthen the robots' voice interaction.

There is also an independent director, Yu Kai, the CEO of Horizon Robotics, who is also an expert in the field of autonomous driving.

> Yu Kai, born November 1976, holds a PhD and is a Chinese national with US permanent residency. He earned his bachelor's and master's degrees in electronic science and engineering at Nanjing University (1998, 2000) and a PhD in computer science from the University of Munich (2004). He was a senior research scientist at Siemens' central research institute (2004–2006), a senior researcher and department head at NEC Labs America (2006–2012), and executive deputy dean of Baidu's Institute of Deep Learning and executive dean of Baidu Research (2012–2015). Since July 2015 he has been CEO of Beijing Horizon Robotics, and he is an independent director of Ecovacs. (Ecovacs 2018 annual report, translated)
