*This analysis was first published in Chinese in May 2026, when Dreame launched the X60 Pro Steam in China.*

Dreame's latest robot vacuum, the X60 Pro Steam, launched in China on 20 May 2026 with a headline feature: 180°C steam for sanitising the floor.

On specification alone it puts almost everything the category can offer into one machine. It mops with a continuously rinsed roller fed with 75°C hot water. The roller extends up to 4 cm towards edges, and the side brush extends as well. It claims 37,000 Pa of suction, 100°C hot-water washing in the dock and high-temperature drying. To that it adds steam: Dreame promotes steam in eight seconds and fast steam-cleaning coverage.

![Underside of the Dreame X60 Pro Steam showing its roller mop releasing steam](/images/articles/is-steam-the-next-robot-vacuum-feature/02-steam-roller.webp)

*Image: Dreame.*

It also has a retractable LiDAR turret to lower the body for low furniture, a cover that protects the roller from wetting carpet, and 5 cm threshold crossing. A 3.2 L dust bag, automatic refilling and draining, and automatic dust emptying are now standard on high-end models.

As a mass-produced machine, the X60 Pro Steam is near the technical ceiling of the category. Robot vacuums were already intensely competitive; Dreame has now put steam on top of fresh-water mopping.

## What it suggests about DJI's next ROMO

Set against DJI's [ROMO 2](/blog/dji-romo-2-launch-analysis), the X60 Pro Steam shows where a ROMO 3 is likely to go. It leads ROMO 2 in four areas:

- **Hot-water mopping.** Not just fresh-water mopping, but heated water. Several leading brands have pushed this since last year, and it is likely to become an important high-end feature.
- **An extending roller.** A fresh-water roller is already a strong cleaning structure; extending it 4 cm addresses edges and corners as well.
- **A roller cover for carpets.** It reduces the risk of wetting rugs. It is useful, but I would rank it below hot water and roller extension.
- **Steam.** The biggest talking point. Steam targets stubborn stains and adds sanitising. One caveat matters: 180°C is likely to be the boiler temperature. After travelling through the pipes to the floor, the steam is probably much cooler, perhaps in the 70s Celsius.

## Will steam become mainstream?

In the short term, I do not think so.

Steam has real appeal. High-temperature sanitising is easy for consumers to understand, steam mops and steam floor washers have long had a steady market, and steam has arrived in floor washers too; Roborock's A30 Pro Steam has sold reasonably well. But a robot vacuum is not a floor washer, and adding steam raises practical problems.

**Space and power.** A robot vacuum has limited room, battery capacity and heating power. It is hard to deliver steady, high-temperature steam for long periods the way dedicated steam products do, so runtime and effect may suffer while costs rise.

**The temperature gap.** Shoppers who hear "180°C" may imagine 100°C-plus steam hitting the floor directly. The real experience is likely to be milder.

**Invisible results.** Robot vacuums mostly run while nobody watches, so users barely notice whether steam made a difference. Fresh-water mopping, by contrast, has a visible logic: wash, scrub and recover dirty water as you go.

**Chemical alternatives.** Many robot vacuums already accept sanitising solution, whose effect is clearer and easier to verify. Steam is an attractive concept, but it may convert to sales less easily than fresh-water mopping.

**Heated cleaning solutions.** If users add cleaning solution to the tank and it passes through a high-temperature boiler, brands need to rule out unexpected chemical reactions and safety risks.

**Scale.** Boilers build up limescale over time. If it is not managed, steam output drops or the system fails, which adds after-sales risk to an already highly integrated product.

**Safety.** The robot runs on its own in homes with pets and children, so the safety of high-temperature steam needs further validation.

Steam is a striking innovation and shows Dreame's willingness to push high-end technology. But hot-water mopping, extending rollers, low bodies, threshold crossing and carpet protection are easier for users to perceive and more likely to become industry standards.

Steam looks more like a flagship differentiator. It lifts a product's technical image and gives brands something memorable for launches and marketing. I do not expect it to spread across the industry the way fresh-water mopping has.

For DJI's next model, I would expect hot-water mopping and an extending roller; a roller cover will depend on positioning; steam is unlikely to be a priority.

The X60 Pro Steam matters less as one fully loaded flagship than as another step in how robot vacuums compete. Whether the industry follows on steam is open. High-end competition has clearly moved beyond suction, obstacle avoidance and docks to overall cleaning capability.
