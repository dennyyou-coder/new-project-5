**Dolphin and Polaris both sell capable robotic pool cleaners, and neither brand is the automatic winner for every pool.** Dolphin is the flagship pool-cleaner brand of Maytronics, a specialist in robotic pool cleaning. Polaris is a Fluidra brand inside a wider pool-equipment, connected-control and dealer network. Both currently offer corded and cordless robots, but their ranges differ by pool size, coverage, filtration, controls, handling and service channel.

The useful decision is therefore not “Dolphin or Polaris?” in the abstract. It is:

> **Which current, destination-market SKU completes the required pool-cleaning mission with the lowest handling, maintenance and support risk?**

This article compares only robotic pool cleaners. Polaris also sells pressure-side and suction-side cleaners, but those depend on different hydraulic architectures and should not be used as evidence for or against its robots.

## Who Is Behind Dolphin and Polaris?

Maytronics says it has developed robotic pool-cleaning systems since 1983 and presents Dolphin as its flagship brand. Its current U.S. range is organized across value, best-seller and high-performance cleaners, as well as in-ground, above-ground and cordless categories. The company also maintains a separate commercial robot range.

Polaris has a different corporate context. Fluidra's official [Polaris brand page](https://www.fluidra.com/brands/polaris/) places the brand in its portfolio and describes robotic, pressure and suction cleaners as separate product families. Fluidra also owns brands such as Zodiac, Jandy and AstralPool and sells a much wider pool-equipment system.

That difference creates two possible channel advantages:

- Dolphin can fit a dealer or service company that wants a dedicated robotic-cleaner line with a specialist brand and model ladder.
- Polaris can fit a pool professional already working inside Fluidra's equipment, controls, parts and dealer ecosystem.

Corporate platform is not a substitute for local service. For the ownership and legal-responsibility chains, see [Who Makes Dolphin Pool Cleaners?](https://worldcleanbiz.com/blog/who-makes-dolphin-pool-cleaners-maytronics) and [Who Owns Polaris Pool Cleaners?](https://worldcleanbiz.com/blog/who-owns-polaris-pool-cleaners-fluidra-zodiac).

## Compare Robots With Robots

The Polaris name is unusually easy to compare incorrectly because it covers several cleaner architectures.

Polaris' current [robotic cleaner category](https://www.polarispool.com/products/pool-cleaners/robotic-pool-cleaners) describes self-contained filtration, independent power and both corded and cordless products. Its pressure-side cleaners instead use pool-water pressure and may require a booster pump. Its suction-side cleaners connect to the pool's circulation system.

Dolphin robots also carry their own drive, brushes, pump and filter. A corded Dolphin uses a poolside power supply and floating low-voltage cable; a cordless model carries a rechargeable battery.

For a fair comparison:

1. match robotic products only;
2. match the same pool class and maximum size;
3. match the required floor, wall and waterline zones;
4. match corded with corded or cordless with cordless unless power architecture is the decision;
5. use the same representative debris and maintenance condition; and
6. include warranty and service in the result.

## Current Product-Range Logic

Both brands have broad ranges, so one familiar model cannot represent the whole brand.

### Dolphin examples

Maytronics currently lists the [Dolphin Nautilus CC](https://maytronics.com/en-us/store/residential-pools/best-value-cleaners/dolphin-nautilus-cc/99996113-USB.html) for pools up to 33 feet with floor-and-wall coverage, top-load filtration and no mobile app. The [Nautilus CC Plus with Wi-Fi](https://maytronics.com/en-us/store/residential-pools/best-performance-cleaners/dolphin-nautilus-cc-plus-w%2Fwi-fi/99996409-PCI.html) increases the stated pool length to 40 feet and adds MyDolphin Plus control, an anti-tangle swivel and ultra-fine filtration on the current U.S. listing.

At the cordless end, the current [Dolphin LIBERTY 400](https://maytronics.com/en-us/store/residential-pools/cordless-cleaners/dolphin-liberty-400/99998140-US.html) lists floor, wall and waterline coverage, interchangeable fine and ultra-fine filtration, magnetic charging and Click-Up retrieval. Those features belong to that SKU and market; they should not be projected onto every Dolphin.

### Polaris examples

Polaris' current U.S. robot page lists a large model ladder spanning value corded products, iQ-connected corded products and several FREEDOM cordless variants.

The [Polaris ALPHA iQ+](https://www.polarispool.com/products/pool-cleaners/robotic-pool-cleaners/alpha-iq-plus-p) is a corded in-ground model with a 70-foot cable, 5-liter illuminated canister, floor/wall/waterline modes, custom in-app scheduling, targeted control and an included caddy. The [Polaris FREEDOM Plus](https://www.polarispool.com/products/pool-cleaners/robotic-pool-cleaners/freedom-plus-cordless-robotic-cleaner-p) is cordless, lists a 4-liter canister and charging under five hours, and adds underwater remote communication for direction and retrieval. Its product page explicitly says scheduling is not included.

These examples show why model matching matters. “App-controlled” can mean a weekly schedule on one corded machine, status and mode selection on a cordless machine, or a narrower feature set on another SKU.

## Corded or Cordless?

Neither brand forces one architecture. Both sell corded and cordless robots.

Corded systems remove the onboard battery and can support repeated scheduled cycles on selected models, but the operator must place the power supply safely, manage the cable and store it correctly. Cordless systems remove the operating cable but add battery capacity, charging time, wet-product retrieval and future battery-service questions.

For a deeper architecture comparison, see [Cordless vs Corded Robotic Pool Cleaners](https://worldcleanbiz.com/blog/cordless-vs-corded-robotic-pool-cleaners).

Buyers should record:

- complete cycle time in the target pool;
- interventions and incomplete zones;
- minutes to deploy and retrieve;
- cable arrangement or charging work;
- filter cleaning time;
- time until the robot is ready for the next mission;
- cable, power-supply, battery, charger and seal service terms.

A cable-free demonstration can look simpler while adding more post-cycle work. A corded robot can appear less convenient while better fitting repeat schedules. Measure the complete mission.

## Floor, Wall, Waterline and Surface Coverage

Coverage is model-specific.

Some entry products clean the floor only. Others climb walls but do not scrub the waterline. Premium models may offer a waterline-only mode or a full floor-wall-waterline cycle. Steps, benches, tanning ledges, sharp transitions, drains and irregular geometry remain separate questions even when a page says “complete coverage.”

Surface skimming is also a different job. A submerged floor-and-wall robot does not automatically remove floating leaves, pollen or insects. Polaris markets Skimbot as a separate surface cleaner, which illustrates the distinction. Do not translate a brand's surface-cleaner product into a capability of every submerged robot.

During a site trial, mark:

- floor zones;
- walls and coves;
- waterline;
- main drains;
- slopes and transitions;
- steps, shelves and ledges;
- high-debris corners.

Run at least three cycles with realistic debris and record missed zones. A single climb in a showroom or video does not establish repeatable coverage.

## Filtration and Debris Handling

Both brands use self-contained filters, but the filters are not interchangeable concepts.

Dolphin listings use terms including fine filter, ultra-fine filter, combined filter and filter bag. Access can be top or bottom depending on model. The current Nautilus CC Plus page specifies top access and an ultra-fine kit; the LIBERTY 400 page describes interchangeable cartridges.

Polaris product pages commonly specify canister volume, transparent lids and, on selected models, a dirty-canister indicator. ALPHA iQ+ currently lists a 5-liter illuminated canister, while FREEDOM Plus lists 4 liters and no dirty-canister indicator.

Do not rank two products from “microns” or liters alone. Ask:

- Is the figure for mesh, a complete cartridge or one optional panel?
- Can the system capture both large leaves and fine silt in the same configuration?
- What happens to flow and pickup as the filter loads?
- Can the operator empty the basket without redistributing debris?
- Are the filter panels, basket, lid, latch and seals individually replaceable?
- How much wet debris can the operator realistically lift and rinse?

A large canister can reduce emptying frequency but add wet weight. A finer filter can improve particle capture but require more frequent cleaning. Test the actual debris.

## Drive, Brushes, Navigation and Main Drains

Marketing names are not cross-brand measurements.

Dolphin uses product terms such as CleverClean and active scrubbing. Polaris uses terms such as Sensor Nav, SMART Cycle, four-wheel drive and Easy Lift on selected models. These describe each company's system; they do not create a standardized score.

The practical trial should measure:

- brush contact on the pool surface;
- pickup of leaves, grit, hair and fine sediment;
- traction on slopes and walls;
- recovery after a drain, obstacle or cable interaction;
- repeated coverage rather than one completed path;
- filter loading and cleaning result at the end of the cycle.

Main-drain behavior is especially pool-specific. Drain height, cover profile, suction, approach angle, robot clearance and traction all matter. Request a trial in the actual pool or a faithful test fixture instead of accepting a universal “will not get stuck” promise.

## App Control and Scheduling

The logo of an app does not define what the robot can do.

On the current corded Dolphin Nautilus CC Plus listing, MyDolphin Plus supports remote start, stop, scheduling and monitoring. On the cordless LIBERTY 400 listing, the app is described for use when the robot is out of the pool. The energy and communication architecture changes the workflow.

Polaris' corded ALPHA iQ+ page lists recurring schedules, remote targeted control, water temperature, a dirty-canister indicator and over-the-air updates through iAquaLink. The cordless FREEDOM Plus page lists charge status, specialized modes and completion notifications; a separate hand-held LiFi remote directs the robot underwater and activates retrieval.

Verify these functions separately:

| Function | Questions to ask |
|---|---|
| Initial setup | Which Wi-Fi band, phone OS, account and region are supported? |
| In-water control | Does the phone communicate directly, through a power supply, or not while submerged? |
| Scheduling | Can the robot remain safely deployed, and how is energy restored? |
| Updates | Who owns the account, how long are updates provided, and what happens if cloud support ends? |
| Shared use | Can a homeowner, service technician and dealer use separate roles? |
| Data | What pool, device and location data are collected and where are they processed? |

For many pools, reliable cleaning and simple controls matter more than an elaborate app. Treat connectivity as a service commitment, not a decorative feature.

## Weight, Storage and Daily Maintenance

Robot dry weight is only one handling input. The operator also lifts retained water and debris, manages a cable or charging caddy, cleans filters and finds a shaded storage location.

Current official pages show wide variation even within one brand. Dolphin's Nautilus CC is listed at under 15 pounds, while the Nautilus CC Plus is about 20.8 pounds. Polaris FREEDOM Plus emphasizes water evacuation at retrieval and includes a charging caddy; ALPHA iQ+ includes a transport caddy and an Easy Lift mode.

During a trial, ask the intended operator to:

1. move the complete packaged system;
2. deploy it safely;
3. retrieve it after a full debris cycle;
4. let it drain;
5. remove and rinse the filter;
6. arrange the cable or charge the battery;
7. store the robot, power equipment and accessories.

If any step is awkward, it becomes a service-call, misuse or non-use risk.

## Warranty, Dealers, Repairs and Parts

Warranty cannot be compared at brand level without the model, market and seller.

Maytronics' current U.S. pages show different warranty periods across Dolphin SKUs, and its [store locator](https://www.maytronics.com/en-us/store-locator/) identifies authorized sales, repair, diagnosis, warranty and parts services. Polaris pages route buyers to a dealer and Fluidra equipment support. The reviewed North American Polaris warranty uses Zodiac Pool Systems LLC, a Fluidra company, and product-category terms.

Before purchase, obtain in writing:

- exact model and serial-number policy;
- warranty period and start date;
- residential, commercial and rental-use exclusions;
- wear-item, filter, brush, track, cable and battery treatment;
- labor, freight and diagnostic charges;
- repair versus replacement rules;
- local service-center address and seasonal turnaround;
- parts numbers, prices and lead times;
- whether unauthorized online channels affect coverage.

An extra year on paper may be worth less than a qualified local repair center during peak season.

## Which Brand Fits Which Buyer?

### Consider Dolphin when:

- the dealer wants a dedicated robotic-cleaner specialist and a broad model ladder;
- a local Maytronics service center supports the exact SKU;
- the selected Dolphin's filtration and coverage match the pool;
- the buyer values a current corded or cordless feature set available in the local channel.

### Consider Polaris when:

- the pool professional already works within Fluidra's dealer and equipment ecosystem;
- iAquaLink integration or a particular Polaris retrieval/control feature is valuable;
- a local Polaris/Fluidra dealer has strong parts and repair capability;
- the selected robot—not a pressure or suction product—matches the required mission.

### Do not choose either brand until:

- the pool has been measured;
- the debris mix and cleaning zones are defined;
- the exact destination-market SKU is identified;
- handling is tested by the actual operator;
- the warranty provider and service path are confirmed;
- the lifecycle parts list is priced.

## FAQ

### Is Dolphin better than Polaris?

Not as a universal brand rule. Compare exact products for the same pool, debris, coverage, handling and service conditions. Either brand can be the better fit for a specific project.

### Are Polaris robotic pool cleaners made by Fluidra?

Polaris is a Fluidra brand. In North America, Zodiac Pool Systems LLC, a Fluidra company, carries important trademark, warranty and support roles. The physical assembly location should still be verified by model and batch.

### Who makes Dolphin pool cleaners?

Maytronics develops and markets Dolphin robotic pool cleaners. Dolphin is its flagship pool-cleaner brand.

### Do Dolphin and Polaris both make cordless robots?

Yes. Both current U.S. ranges include corded and cordless robotic products. Battery runtime, charging, retrieval, app functions and coverage vary by SKU.

### Which brand has better filtration?

There is no defensible brand-wide answer. Dolphin and Polaris both offer multiple filter configurations. Compare the exact basket or cartridge, debris capacity, access, fine-particle capture, flow under load and cleaning time with the target debris.

### Which app is better: MyDolphin Plus or iAquaLink?

Compare functions on the selected robots, not the app names alone. Corded and cordless products can support different commands, schedules and communication paths. Also compare account ownership, update support and local troubleshooting.

### Can either robot clean steps and tanning ledges?

Do not assume complete step or ledge coverage from a floor-wall-waterline claim. Geometry, depth, traction and transitions vary. Test the exact robot in the actual pool.

### Which brand is easier to repair?

The answer is local. Verify the authorized service center, diagnostics, parts inventory, labor, freight and turnaround for the exact model. A strong nearby dealer can be more important than a brand-level reputation.

## Final Verdict

Dolphin offers the focused logic of a robotic-pool-cleaner specialist. Polaris offers robotic products inside Fluidra's wider pool-equipment and dealer platform. Both have credible corded and cordless options, connected products, self-contained filtration and models that clean floors, walls and waterlines.

The winning product is the one that proves coverage in the target pool, handles its real debris, fits the operator's daily workflow and has a repair route that remains practical after the sale. Compare SKU against SKU, write the service terms down and let a representative pool trial settle the performance question.
