**RIDGID is part of Emerson through Ridge Tool Company. For RIDGID wet/dry vacuums, current patent and warranty evidence also points to Emerson’s tool business. Home Depot is a major retailer and return channel, not the owner of RIDGID.**

That is the short answer. The complete answer requires separating six roles: brand, corporate owner, product business, manufacturer, retailer and warranty operator. They can sit in the same group, but they are not interchangeable.

![A generic wet dry vacuum connecting a corporate owner, engineering and manufacturing operation, retail channel and service network](/images/blog/who-owns-ridgid-wet-dry-vacuums-emerson.webp)

## The Relationship in One Table

| Layer | Entity or evidence | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Brand | RIDGID | The product identity and trademark system seen by customers |
| Core company | Ridge Tool Company | The company behind the RIDGID history and professional-tool business |
| Parent group | Emerson Electric Co. | The corporate group that includes Ridge Tool Company |
| Wet/dry-vac product evidence | Emerson Tool Co. patent list and RIDGID warranty | Connects current RIDGID vacuum models, patents and after-sales handling to Emerson’s tool organization |
| Retail channel | Home Depot and other retailers | Sell products and apply their own return or exchange policies |
| Exact manufacturing site | Model-specific evidence required | Must be verified from the ordered SKU, label, factory and compliance documents |

This distinction prevents two common errors: calling Home Depot the owner because it is a prominent seller, or assuming one corporate answer identifies the factory behind every RIDGID product.

## Ridge Tool Company and Emerson

RIDGID’s [official company timeline](https://www.ridgid.com/us/en/ridgidbuilds-timeline) says Ridge Tool Company was incorporated in Elyria, Ohio, in 1923. It says Ridge Tool became part of Emerson Electric in 1966.

The current corporate evidence is equally direct. RIDGID’s [privacy notice](https://www.ridgid.com/us/en/privacy-notice), revised January 15, 2026, describes Ridge Tool Company and other Emerson Professional Tools entities as subsidiaries of Emerson Electric Co.

Emerson’s [fiscal 2025 subsidiary exhibit](https://ir.emerson.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000032604-25-000087/exhibit21fy25.htm) also lists Ridge Tool Company, Ridge Tool Manufacturing Company, RIDGID, Inc. and related entities inside the Emerson structure.

For a brand-ownership question, this is stronger evidence than a retailer page, reseller biography or logo comparison. It comes from the brand’s own legal notice, its company history and the parent company’s regulatory filing.

## Who Is Connected to RIDGID Wet/Dry Vacuums?

RIDGID’s [official About page](https://www.ridgid.com/us/en/about-ridgid) says its US patent lists for certain RIDGID tools and for wet/dry vacuums, filters and accessories are provided by Emerson.

The linked [2026 Emerson Tool Co. virtual patent marking document](https://www.emerson.com/is/content/emerson/en/corporate/safety-and-productivity/emerson-tool-company/documents/ALL-VACS_Patent-Virtual-Marking_REV-O_20260102.pdf) lists many RIDGID wet/dry vacuum models, including corded, cordless, backpack and professional units.

That supports a specific conclusion: Emerson’s tool organization has a current product and intellectual-property role in the RIDGID wet/dry vacuum line.

It does not prove that the Emerson Tool Co. address is the physical factory for every listed model. Patent marking identifies a product and patent relationship, not necessarily the assembly plant, country of origin, importer or component supplier.

## Why Home Depot Is Often Mistaken for the Owner

RIDGID wet/dry vacuums have a strong retail association with Home Depot in the United States. Repeated exposure in one channel can make a retailer look like a brand owner even when the legal roles are different.

The [RIDGID wet/dry vac warranty](https://www.ridgid.com/us/en/wet-dry-vac-warranty) separates those roles. It says a vacuum bought within 90 days may be returned to Home Depot with the receipt for replacement. It then says other retailers have their own return or exchange policies. After the retailer’s return window, claims go through “us (manufacturer)” for inspection and warranty handling.

This is a useful operational map:

- Home Depot handles its retail sale and stated early return path;
- other retailers apply their own policies;
- the product organization handles eligible manufacturer-warranty claims after the retail return period.

Home Depot’s commercial importance does not make it the owner of RIDGID.

## Brand Licensing Does Not Change the Wet/Dry-Vac Answer

The RIDGID name appears on more than one product family. That makes licensing relevant, but it also creates opportunities for overgeneralization.

In a [2003 Emerson filing](https://ir.emerson.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000950138-03-000298/0000950138-03-000298.pdf), Emerson said it would discontinue manufacturing bench-top and stationary woodworking power tools and develop a program to license the RIDGID brand to manufacturers that could serve future Home Depot requirements.

The same disclosure drew an important boundary. Emerson said it would remain a significant manufacturer of RIDGID wet/dry vacuums, hand tools and other products for Home Depot. It also said its professional contractor and plumbing tools were a separate Emerson business.

The filing is historical, so it should not be treated as a complete map of every 2026 supply relationship. Its value is structural: even inside one brand, a licensed woodworking tool, a professional plumbing tool and a wet/dry vacuum can follow different manufacturing and commercial arrangements.

## Ownership, Manufacturing and Retail Are Different Questions

Use this formula when investigating any tool brand:

> Brand relationship = trademark owner + product manufacturer or licensee + retailer + warranty operator

The terms answer different questions:

1. **Brand owner:** Who controls the brand and corporate value?
2. **Trademark owner:** Which legal entity owns a particular mark in a jurisdiction?
3. **Licensee:** Who has permission to use the mark for defined products or markets?
4. **Manufacturer:** Which legal entity and plant make the exact model?
5. **Retailer:** Who sells it to the customer?
6. **Warranty operator:** Who receives, evaluates and settles claims?

A product can show one brand, be made by another entity, sold by a third and supported by a fourth. The contract and label—not the brand color—determine the commercial responsibility.

## What Can Be Confirmed About the Factory?

The available corporate, patent and warranty records establish the Emerson relationship. They do not establish one factory for all RIDGID vacuums.

To verify an exact model, request or inspect:

- full model and regional variant;
- rating label and country-of-origin marking;
- carton and importer information;
- owner’s manual and warranty issuer;
- legal manufacturer and factory address;
- current safety report and certificate applicant;
- critical-component list;
- factory audit and production-flow evidence;
- purchase contract and change-control terms.

Different tank sizes, cordless platforms or retail bundles may come from different production arrangements. A listing for one model cannot safely be extended to the entire line.

## The Warranty Is Also a Product-Scope Warning

RIDGID’s current vacuum warranty says covered vacuums are designed for a manufacturer-defined run life of 500 to 700 hours. It also distinguishes vacuum coverage from the warranty used for power tools.

This matters for two reasons.

First, “full lifetime warranty” does not mean an unlimited calendar promise under every use condition. Buyers need to read the manufacturer’s definition, exclusions and claim path.

Second, a warranty attached to RIDGID wet/dry vacuums should not be copied to a different RIDGID category. Product scope matters as much as brand identity.

For distributors, the warranty review should cover:

- commercial versus household use;
- wear items such as hoses and filters;
- blockage or motor-burnout exclusions;
- proof-of-purchase requirements;
- replacement versus repair;
- who pays outbound and return freight;
- service stock and discontinued-model support.

## Why the Parts Ecosystem Matters

Wet/dry vacuums are durable platforms supported by filters, bags, hoses, adapters, nozzles and replacement assemblies. The installed base can create recurring parts revenue, but only if compatibility is controlled.

Retailers and distributors should map:

- vacuum model and serial range;
- filter type and dust class;
- collection-bag fit;
- hose diameter and locking interface;
- extension-wand and accessory compatibility;
- motor-head or tank interchangeability;
- region-specific electrical parts;
- active, superseded and discontinued references.

The owner of a brand may set the platform strategy, while the practical customer experience depends on model-level parts data and local inventory.

## What This Means for Consumers

Consumers can use a simple rule:

- treat Emerson and Ridge Tool as the ownership context;
- treat Home Depot as a retailer and early return route when applicable;
- use the exact product warranty for claims;
- use the model number, not only the RIDGID name, to buy filters and accessories.

If country of origin matters, inspect the specific product label or current retailer documentation. Corporate ownership alone cannot answer it.

## What This Means for Distributors and Suppliers

For a distributor, the ownership answer helps identify the group behind the brand, but it is only the first step. The commercial file still needs:

- legal seller and importer;
- territory and channel authorization;
- current price and promotion rules;
- warranty and return allocation;
- spare-parts availability;
- compliance holder and factory;
- product-liability allocation;
- model and supplier change control.

For an OEM or component supplier, do not approach “RIDGID” as if it were a single undifferentiated buying entity. Identify the product category, business unit, model platform, region and responsible legal entity.

The same discipline applies to competitor research. Compare the evidence chain behind a product, not only the brand displayed on its tank.

## A Practical Verification Checklist

Before publishing, buying or quoting a claim about RIDGID vacuums, verify:

10. Are filters, bags, hoses and spare parts mapped to the model?

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Home Depot own RIDGID?

No. Home Depot is an important RIDGID retail channel. RIDGID sits within Emerson through Ridge Tool Company.

### Does Emerson make RIDGID wet/dry vacuums?

Current patent and warranty evidence connects RIDGID wet/dry vacuums to Emerson’s tool organization. A 2003 Emerson filing also described Emerson as a significant manufacturer of the category. The exact factory and origin still require model-level verification.

### Is every RIDGID product made by the same company?

No such conclusion should be drawn. RIDGID spans professional tools, power tools, wet/dry vacuums and other products, and Emerson’s own historical disclosure describes different licensing and business arrangements across categories.

### Are RIDGID vacuums made in the United States?

Ownership evidence does not answer country of origin. Check the rating label, carton, manual, importer and current compliance file for the exact model and market.

### Who handles a RIDGID vacuum warranty claim?

The current US warranty says eligible purchases within Home Depot’s stated early return window may go back to the retailer. After the retailer’s return period, the manufacturer warranty process applies. Always check the exact product and market terms.

### Is RIDGID owned by a Chinese company?

The current ownership evidence identifies Ridge Tool Company as part of US-listed Emerson Electric Co. Do not confuse RIDGID with Shop-Vac, which has a different ownership chain.
