**Choose Dolphin when a current, clearly documented robotic-cleaner range, app control and a dedicated Maytronics sales and service route matter most. Choose Hayward when the local pool professional can support a suitable Hayward robot inside a broader equipment, parts and service relationship.**

The decision must be made model by model. This comparison uses the current Dolphin Explorer E50 page and Hayward's official TigerShark QC documentation as representative corded robotic platforms. It does not compare Hayward suction-side or pressure-side cleaners, which use different hydraulic systems.

## Compare Robots With Robots

Hayward sells several pool-cleaner architectures. A suction-side cleaner connects to the pool's suction plumbing. A pressure-side cleaner uses return-side pressure and may require a booster pump. A robotic cleaner carries its own low-voltage drive, pump and filter.

Dolphin is primarily recognized as a robotic-cleaner brand. Its corded robots use a poolside power supply and floating cable; cordless products use an onboard battery.

For a fair Dolphin–Hayward comparison:

1. select robotic models only;
2. match pool size and surface;
3. match floor, wall and waterline requirements;
4. compare the same debris;
5. include cable, filter and retrieval work;
6. confirm local service before purchase.

The [robotic, suction and pressure cleaner guide](/blog/robotic-vs-suction-vs-pressure-pool-cleaners) explains the underlying architectures.

## Product Platform and Company Context

Dolphin is the flagship pool-cleaner brand of Maytronics. Maytronics has built its sales, software, filtration and repair network around robotic pool cleaning and presents a large residential model ladder.

Hayward is a wider pool-equipment company. Its portfolio covers pumps, filters, heating, sanitization, automation and cleaners. Hayward has used AquaVac and TigerShark names in its robotic-cleaner business.

That creates two legitimate dealer strategies:

- a specialist Dolphin relationship centered on robotic cleaning;
- a Hayward relationship that can combine the cleaner with other pool equipment and an existing service account.

Corporate breadth does not guarantee better robot support. A dealer may stock Hayward pumps but not diagnose a TigerShark power supply. Ask for the exact robotic-cleaner capability.

## Pool Coverage and Navigation

Both reference products are specified for floor, wall and waterline cleaning. Hayward also names coves in the TigerShark coverage.

Those words do not guarantee every square foot. Pool geometry changes robot behavior:

- main drains and raised covers;
- steep transitions;
- benches and tanning ledges;
- narrow corners;
- stairs;
- slippery tile;
- irregular free-form shapes;
- strong circulation flow.

Dolphin markets navigation and enhanced cycles through its current controller and app. Hayward's manual describes Adaptive Seek Control Logic for the TigerShark platform.

Neither term is a standardized coverage score. Test repeated cycles and mark missed zones. A robot that reaches the waterline once has not demonstrated repeatable waterline cleaning.

## Cycle Time: Faster Is Not Automatically Better

Dolphin lists 1.5-, 2- and 2.5-hour cycles for the Explorer E50. Hayward lists a four-hour standard cycle and a 90-minute Quick Clean for TigerShark QC.

Cycle length should be matched to the mission:

- light daily debris;
- post-storm leaves;
- fine silt;
- algae film;
- floor-only touch-up;
- full floor, wall and waterline cleaning.

A shorter cycle can reduce waiting and energy use but may leave difficult zones. A longer cycle can provide more passes but also adds cable movement, filter loading and wear.

For a site trial, record debris removed, missed zones and filter condition—not only whether the cycle finished.

## Filtration and Debris Handling

Explorer E50 uses a top-access multi-layer filtration system. Maytronics also lists a full-filter indicator and a nominal suction rate of 4,000 gph.

TigerShark uses removable, reusable pleated filter cartridges. Hayward's instructions tell owners to clean the cartridges after each use or as often as needed. Hayward also offers a spring-cleanup filter option for larger debris in the official sell sheet.

The useful test uses the actual pool debris:

- large leaves;
- flower fragments;
- insects;
- hair;
- sand;
- fine silt;
- soft organic material.

Measure pickup when the filter is partly loaded. A clean-filter demonstration can overstate real performance.

Also compare the handling sequence:

1. open the robot;
2. remove the basket or cartridges;
3. dispose of debris;
4. hose the filter;
5. inspect seals and latches;
6. reinstall without bypass.

Top access is convenient only if the wet basket remains easy to lift and rinse. Cartridges are useful only if replacements remain available.

## Brushes, Tracks and Surface Compatibility

Explorer E50 lists two brushes with active scrubbing. TigerShark uses a tracked direct-drive system and brushes the pool during its programmed routine.

Surface compatibility is model- and roller-specific. Hayward's TigerShark sheet lists gunite, vinyl, fiberglass, pebble and tile, with a note that tile may require a foam-roller option.

Before purchase, confirm:

- pool finish;
- brush or roller material;
- traction on the waterline tile;
- risk around damaged plaster or vinyl;
- replacement brush and track part numbers;
- installation procedure.

One brand can perform differently across two pools because surface friction and geometry change.

## Cable, Power Supply and Storage

Both reference systems are corded. That avoids an onboard traction battery but creates a cable-management system.

Check:

- GFCI-protected outlet;
- required power-supply distance from the pool;
- cable length and swivel;
- cable memory and coiling;
- shaded dry storage;
- connector condition;
- caddy availability;
- ability to replace the cable separately.

The TigerShark manual specifies a 55-ft floating cable and instructs the owner to keep the power supply at least 10 feet from the pool edge. Explorer E50 lists an anti-tangle swivel and includes a caddy on the current product comparison.

Do not lift a robot by its cable. Allow water to drain and use the handle as instructed.

## App Control and Simple Controls

Explorer E50 uses MyDolphin Plus for schedules, cycle choices and spot cleaning. Connected controls can reduce visits to the power supply and make scheduled cleaning easier.

App value depends on the complete service:

- Wi-Fi coverage near the equipment pad;
- account setup;
- supported phone operating system;
- regional cloud availability;
- ownership transfer;
- software-support duration;
- functions available while the robot is underwater.

TigerShark QC's proposition is more direct: choose the standard or Quick Clean routine from the power system and let the onboard logic operate.

Simple controls can be an advantage for a service route with many pools. App control can be an advantage for homeowners who want schedules and mode selection. Neither approach proves better cleaning.

## Maintenance and Repair

Routine maintenance includes:

- cleaning the filter after each cycle;
- removing hair from brush and track ends;
- checking the cable and swivel;
- rinsing the robot with fresh water;
- inspecting latches and seals;
- storing the unit out of direct weather;
- following water-chemistry limits in the manual.

The high-value repair parts are often different from consumables:

- power supply;
- floating cable and connector;
- motor block;
- drive motor;
- pump;
- control board;
- tracks and bearings;
- filter housing.

Ask whether the local service center repairs modules or replaces the complete robot. Obtain seasonal turnaround, diagnostic fee and freight terms.

Maytronics exposes warranty registration, manuals and repair requests through its [Dolphin support page](https://www.maytronics.com/en-us/support-dolphin.html). Hayward buyers should confirm that the selected dealer services robotic cleaners, not only the company's pumps and filters.

Dolphin can fit a dealer that wants:

Hayward can fit a dealer that wants:

- one broader pool-equipment account;

The dealer should calculate five-year value from margin, warranty labor, parts sales, return rate and repair turnaround. The checkout price alone does not show channel fit.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Dolphin better than Hayward?

Not for every pool. Dolphin has a highly visible current robot range and dedicated Maytronics support. Hayward can be a good fit where the exact robot and local dealer support are confirmed.

### Does Hayward make robotic pool cleaners?

Yes. Hayward has marketed robotic platforms including TigerShark and AquaVac. Do not confuse them with Hayward suction-side or pressure-side cleaners.

### Which cleaner has the shorter cycle?

Explorer E50 lists 1.5-, 2- and 2.5-hour cycles. TigerShark QC lists a 90-minute Quick Clean and a four-hour standard cycle.

### Do both clean the waterline?

The two cited reference models list waterline coverage. Actual performance depends on surface, geometry, filter loading and traction.

### Which filtration system is easier to clean?

Dolphin E50 uses a top-load basket; TigerShark uses removable pleated cartridges. The better workflow depends on debris and access. Test both with wet material.

### Does the TigerShark QC have an app?

The cited TigerShark QC documentation emphasizes power-supply cycle controls, not an app. Verify newer or regional Hayward models separately.

### Can a Hayward pool-equipment dealer repair the robot?

Not automatically. Confirm that the location handles robotic-cleaner diagnostics, warranty labor and parts for the exact model.

## The Bottom Line

Dolphin and Hayward represent two different buying routes.

Dolphin offers a current specialist robot ladder with detailed product pages, connected controls and a dedicated Maytronics support path. Hayward can integrate the cleaner into a broader pool-equipment relationship, but the buyer should verify current model availability and robot-specific service.

Choose from the pool outward: geometry, surface and debris first; filtration and handling second; local parts and repair third. The logo should be the final tie-breaker, not the first decision.
