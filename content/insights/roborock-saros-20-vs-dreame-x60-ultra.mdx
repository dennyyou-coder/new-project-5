**Choose the Roborock Saros 20 when uneven room transitions and low furniture are the main reason rooms go unfinished, and you want adjustable mop pressure. Choose the Dreame X60 Ultra when you want a similarly slim robot with larger published dock capacities and a clearly stated single-threshold rating, and the larger dock fits your space.**

Both robots were announced at CES on 6 January 2026, and both target the same problem: getting a full vacuum-and-mop robot under low furniture and over raised transitions. On paper they are unusually close. The differences that matter sit in how each climbs, how each mops and what the dock asks of the owner.

## Low Furniture: Effectively a Tie

The two bodies differ by 0.3 mm on paper: 79.8 mm for Saros 20 and 79.5 mm for X60 Ultra with its VersaLift sensor retracted. Neither figure guarantees passage. Roborock describes limited passive compression for a lower entry and warns that the robot may scrape; Dreame's raised sensor height is 102.8 mm.

Measure the lowest point under each sofa or bed, including crossbars, and leave a margin for uneven floors. If a piece of furniture sits within a few millimetres of either figure, treat both robots as uncertain rather than picking the one with the smaller number.

## Thresholds: Read the Test Conditions

This is where the robots differ most, and where the headline numbers are easiest to misread.

**Saros 20** uses the AdaptiLift 3.0 chassis. Roborock's double-layer claim is 4.5 cm plus 4.3 cm, but only where the gap between the two steps is greater than 12 cm and no more than 14 cm. It is not an 8.8 cm vertical step rating, and Roborock does not present it as one.

**X60 Ultra** lists 4.5 cm for a single threshold and 8.8 cm for a double-layer threshold. A double-layer figure again describes two smaller steps, not one tall wall.

In practice, both handle a single 4.5 cm lip on paper. Saros 20's case is strongest where the home has stepped transitions that match its tested geometry. Photograph and measure your worst doorway before deciding; the shape of a transition matters as much as its total height.

## Vacuuming: Suction Claims Will Not Decide It

Roborock lists 36,000 Pa and Dreame 35,000 Pa. Both are peak manufacturer figures measured under each company's own conditions. A 1,000 Pa gap between two different machines does not establish which picks up more from your carpet.

More useful differences sit around the suction figure. Dreame uses its HyperStream DuoBrush 2.0 brush system, lists a 235 ml robot dust box and a 3.2 L dock bag, and a 6,400 mAh battery. Pet hair can fill the robot bin before the dock bag needs replacing, so the bin size matters in shedding homes. Roborock publishes about 2.5 hours of charging time but not a comparable bin figure on the profile we checked.

## Mopping: Pressure Control vs Pad Heating

Both robots mop with dual rotating pads, and both docks wash the pads with water at up to 100°C. That temperature describes the dock's washing process, not the water on your floor; Dreame separately describes 40°C warm-water mopping.

**Saros 20** runs its pads at 200 rpm with 8 N standard pressure, rising to 13 N when the higher-pressure setting is enabled in the app. The dock dries the pads at up to 55°C. On carpet, it detaches its mops and raises its chassis, though deep-carpet behaviour needs configuring in the app.

**X60 Ultra** uses removable thermal pads. Check how carpet-first and vacuum-only modes behave with your rugs, because the mop arrangement determines whether wet pads touch carpet.

## The Dock: Capacity vs Footprint

The docks are similar in size: 381 × 475 × 488 mm for the Saros 20 tank dock against 390 × 423 × 499 mm for X60 Ultra. Leave room above for lifting tanks out and in front for the ramp.

Dreame publishes more of the numbers an owner lives with: a 4.2 L clean-water tank, a 3.0 L used-water tank and a 3.2 L dust bag. Its US manual asks for the used-water tank to be cleaned after every use and suggests replacing mop pads every one to three months, the dust-box filter every three to six months and main brushes every six to twelve months.

Both brands offer a plumbed option, but differently. Roborock sells a refill-and-drain dock version, 300 mm tall, in selected markets only. Dreame sells a separate hookup kit. Either way, confirm power, water supply and drainage at the installation point before ordering.

## Price

On 6 September 2026 the official US stores listed Saros 20 with the tank dock at USD 1,299.99 (with a displayed reference of USD 1,599.99) and X60 Ultra at USD 1,499.99. These are dated snapshots. Promotions change often, and bundles with extra accessories carry their own prices.

Add consumables to the comparison: bags, filters, brushes, mop pads and cleaning solution for two years, at your local prices.

## Which One Should You Buy?

### Choose Roborock Saros 20 when:

- stepped transitions match its tested double-layer geometry;
- you want to raise mop pressure for stubborn residue;
- automatic mop detachment on carpet is important;
- the official US price snapshot is lower in your market as well.

### Choose Dreame X60 Ultra when:

- a clearly stated 4.5 cm single threshold covers your home;
- larger published tank and bag capacities suit your routine;
- you want a slightly lighter robot to carry between floors;
- the dock's footprint fits the space you have.

Neither is the right purchase for a level home where a cheaper robot already reaches every room. ## FAQ

### Which is lower, Saros 20 or X60 Ultra?

On paper X60 Ultra is 0.3 mm lower: 79.5 mm with its sensor retracted against 79.8 mm for Saros 20. That difference is too small to rely on. Measure your furniture and leave a margin.

### Can either robot climb an 8.8 cm step?

No. Saros 20's 4.5 + 4.3 cm claim and X60 Ultra's 8.8 cm double-layer claim both describe two smaller steps, not one 8.8 cm vertical step.

### Which has stronger suction?

Roborock claims 36,000 Pa and Dreame 35,000 Pa. Both are peak laboratory figures under different conditions, so the gap does not show which cleans better in a real home.

### Do they mop with 100°C water?

No. Both 100°C figures describe hot-water washing of the mop pads in the dock. Dreame describes 40°C warm-water mopping on the floor.

### Do they need a water connection?

No. Both work with tank docks. Roborock offers a refill-and-drain dock in selected markets, and Dreame sells an optional hookup kit.
