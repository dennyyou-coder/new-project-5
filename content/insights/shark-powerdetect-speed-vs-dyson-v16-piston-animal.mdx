**Choose the Shark PowerDetect Speed Clean & Empty IA3241 when automatic emptying and charging, a compact hand-vac workflow, a bending wand and a lower system price are the priorities. Choose the Dyson V16 Piston Animal when its 315 AW motor, dual-conical anti-tangle floorhead, large compression bin, longer published runtime and detailed sensing justify manual emptying and a premium price.**

This is a workflow comparison as much as a cleaning comparison. Shark puts the stick vacuum into an auto-empty base after each job. Dyson gives the user a larger compressing bin and stronger published motor but currently expects that bin to be emptied by hand.

## Confirm the Exact SKU

Shark sells several PowerDetect products. This article covers IA3241, the PowerDetect Speed Clean & Empty System. Do not copy features from the larger IP3251 PowerDetect Clean & Empty.

The IA3241 product comparison lists three detection functions: Floor, Dirt and Direction. The larger model also lists Edge Detect. Some launch language can blur this distinction, so the model page and carton should control the quotation.

Dyson sells:

- V16 Piston Animal dry vacuum;
- V16 Piston Animal with Floor Dok;
- V16 Piston Animal Submarine wet-and-dry package.

The standard dry V16 does not include the Submarine 2.0 wet roller. Dyson also announced a compatible self-emptying station as a future accessory, but it is not the auto-empty dock included with the current Shark system. Do not compare a planned Dyson accessory with Shark’s in-box base.

Retailers should list full model numbers, floorheads, tools, stand/dock, spare battery and warranty. Names alone are insufficient.

## Floor Cleaning and Agitation

Shark uses the compact TurboPro Detect nozzle. It is 8.3 inches wide and designed to pick up during both forward and reverse travel. Floor Detect changes brush speed across surfaces, Dirt Detect increases power in dirtier areas and Direction Detect supports rearward pickup.

Reverse pickup can reduce the number of corrective passes in a small apartment or around furniture. Test it with hard granules and hair rather than assuming every backward pull captures equally well.

Dyson’s All Floor Cones Sense head uses two conical brush bars. The cones move long hair from the thicker center toward the narrow outer ends, where suction carries it into the bin. The head senses floor changes and adjusts both suction and brush speed.

The Dyson head is intended to combine hard-floor and carpet tasks without changing the main head. Its illumination reveals fine material on hard floors. Shark also uses a blue Reveal light for contrast.

For the same home, test:

- fine dust on hard floor;
- cereal-size particles;
- carpet embedded soil;
- rug-edge snowplowing;
- forward and reverse passes;
- wall approach and inward corner;
- long hair and pet hair;
- debris left after one and two passes.

Shark’s lighter, narrower head may feel agile in small rooms. Dyson’s conical head and stronger published suction may favor demanding hair and carpet tasks. Only a controlled floor test can confirm the result.

## Suction Numbers Are Not the Whole Decision

Dyson publishes 315 Air Watts from a 900 W Hyperdymium motor. Dynamic Cyclone adds five cyclones in Boost mode. That is a clear manufacturer specification.

Shark does not publish a directly comparable Air Watt number on the IA3241 product page. Instead, it supports its lightweight debris-cleaning claim with ASTM F2607 and IEC 62885-2 test references.

Do not convert motor wattage into Air Watts or compare an airflow-independent marketing claim with Dyson’s maximum AW as though the methods match.

Real pickup also depends on floorhead seal, brush contact, airflow under filter load and the selected mode. Test both vacuums with clean filters, then repeat after several normal cleaning cycles.

## Hair Handling

Shark calls the TurboPro brushroll self-cleaning and anti-hair-wrap. Its value should be measured at the roller ends and bearings, not only across the visible center.

Dyson’s dual cones are an explicit mechanical answer to long hair. Dyson states that the head can detangle strands up to 25 inches in its test. V16 also includes or supports a Hair Screw tool depending on bundle for upholstery and stairs.

Run a controlled hair test:

1. place equal long strands across hard floor and carpet;
2. clean in normal automatic mode;
3. inspect the roller center and both ends;
4. inspect the airway and bin inlet;
5. empty or dock the machine;
6. inspect the disposal path.

A floorhead that avoids tangles can still deliver a tight hair bundle into a bin or auto-empty duct. The whole path matters.

## Auto-Empty Dock vs Compression Bin

Shark’s decisive convenience feature is the 2 L dock. Returning the vacuum triggers automatic dust transfer and charging. The base is marketed to hold up to 45 days of debris.

That can keep the small 0.5 qt onboard cup ready for quick cleaning. It also adds floor space, a dust-transfer motor, filters and an emptying noise event. The 45-day claim varies with home size and debris.

Dyson’s CleanCompaktor keeps dust in the stick vacuum. A lever mechanically compresses debris, increasing the effective 0.35-gallon capacity. Dyson says it can hold 30 days under its test and wipes the inside surfaces as the bin is ejected.

Compression avoids a large auto-empty base and transfer motor. The user still takes the vacuum to a trash container and operates the eject mechanism. Dense dust and hair can create a larger release event than frequent small emptying.

Compare:

- floor area required for storage;
- noise after every dock return;
- dust exposure during base/bin emptying;
- frequency of filter cleaning;
- hair transfer and blockage;
- whether the user can carry the base bin outdoors;
- replacement filters and seals;
- cleaning time after one month.

The numbers “45 days” and “30 days” are not directly comparable because one describes a dock and the other a compressed onboard bin.

## Weight and Handling

Dyson lists the V16 at 7.5 lb.

Shark marketing describes the Speed as a 7 lb design and lists a 3.5 lb hand-vac weight. The IA3241 specification table also shows “Product Weight: 14.2 lbs,” likely reflecting a broader system or packaging definition. Because the page uses multiple weight scopes, buyers should not quote only the lowest number without context.

Try both vacuums:

- on floor for ten minutes;
- in handheld mode on stairs;
- above shoulder height;
- under a bed;
- around chair legs;
- while removing and replacing tools;
- when returning to storage.

Shark’s MultiFLEX wand bends for low furniture and folds for storage. That can reduce back bending. Dyson provides remote cleaner-head release and built-in tools in the wand/body for faster task changes.

Balance matters as much as total weight. A heavy motor and bin high on the handle can fatigue the wrist even if the floorhead supports most mass during straight cleaning.

## Runtime and Charging

Shark publishes up to 60 minutes, measured at the hand vacuum in ECO mode with a non-motorized tool. Its page lists a six-hour charge time.

Dyson publishes up to 70 minutes and a 3.5-hour charge time. It uses a click-in battery, and additional compatible batteries can extend a long session.

Motorized floorheads, carpet, dirt boost and maximum suction reduce runtime. Test the actual mode:

- start at 100%;
- clean the normal floor mix;
- record mode changes and boost events;
- note active cleaning time;
- finish at the real state of charge;
- record recovery to full.

The Shark dock encourages frequent short top-ups after every job. Dyson’s faster listed charge and swappable battery may fit longer planned sessions.

## Filtration and Dust Exposure

Shark states that its Anti-Allergen Complete Seal captures 99.97% of particles 0.3 microns or larger under ASTM F1977, with “allergens” defined as non-living matter. The dock separates routine user contact from the small onboard cup.

Dyson publishes fully sealed five-stage HEPA filtration capturing 99.99% down to 0.1 microns under its conditions.

The figures use different thresholds and should not be treated as a simple percentage race. Evaluate:

- seals around bin and filter;
- filter load and washing interval;
- exhaust leakage;
- auto-empty transfer leakage;
- dust cloud during final disposal;
- availability and price of replacement filters.

Neither vacuum should be presented as medical treatment. Allergy-sensitive users should consider the complete use and disposal process.

## Display, Sensors and Maintenance Guidance

Shark’s intelligence is mostly automatic: floor, dirt and direction sensing adjust operation without requiring an app. The product has illumination and mode controls but does not make connected-app ownership central to the proposition.

Dyson counts microscopic particles up to 15,000 times per second, adjusts power and reports data on its LCD. The MyDyson app offers setup, maintenance alerts and care guidance.

Some users value visual evidence and reminders. Others prefer a simpler appliance without account setup. A retailer should demonstrate both workflows instead of assuming more data always creates more value.

On 2026-07-26, the US product pages showed temporary prices around $399.99 for Shark IA3241 and $849.99 for the Dyson V16 dry model, with both pages also showing higher reference prices or changing stock messages. Those are dated snapshots, not permanent list prices.

Value should include:

- floorhead result on the home’s floors;
- time saved by auto-emptying;
- weight and reach;
- runtime and charge recovery;
- dust exposure;
- parts and filter cost;
- battery replacement;

Shark’s system can be better value when the dock meaningfully reduces daily friction. Dyson can justify its premium when the stronger cleaning architecture, long-hair head and larger onboard capacity reduce passes and maintenance.

Stock Shark IA3241 when:

- auto-empty is a strong demonstration feature;

- a 7 lb marketed design and MultiFLEX address handling objections;
- floor space exists for the dock;

- long-hair management is a major need;

## Final Decision

Choose Shark IA3241 for the lower-friction daily routine: pick it up, clean, return it and let the base empty and charge. Accept the larger storage station, transfer noise, six-hour listed charge and lower published maximum runtime.

Choose Dyson V16 for a more powerful, information-rich vacuum with a novel hair-removing head and large compression bin. Accept the heavier premium purchase, manual disposal and a new tool ecosystem.

If stairs, shoulder-level cleaning or wrist strain are central, handle both in person. If carpet and hair are central, run the same debris test. If dust exposure is central, observe the final disposal—not just the filter specification.

## FAQ

### Is Shark PowerDetect Speed better than Dyson V16?

It is better for buyers who prioritize included auto-emptying, a compact hand-vac workflow, MultiFLEX and lower price. Dyson is stronger on published suction, compression capacity, runtime and its dual-conical hair head.

### Does Dyson V16 auto-empty?

The standard V16 Piston Animal dry package does not include an auto-empty dock. Dyson announced a compatible station as a future accessory, but current bundle contents must be verified at purchase.

### Which is lighter?

Dyson lists 7.5 lb. Shark markets a 7 lb design and lists a 3.5 lb hand vac, while the same page also lists 14.2 lb product weight. Because the scopes differ, try both and confirm what each retailer includes.

### Which runs longer?

Dyson publishes up to 70 minutes; Shark up to 60 minutes. Shark’s figure is explicitly measured in ECO with a non-motorized tool. Motorized heads, carpet and boost reduce both.

### Which is better for long hair?

Dyson’s dual-conical head is specifically designed to move long hair off the rollers. Shark uses a self-cleaning anti-wrap brushroll. Test roller ends, bearings, bin and disposal path with the same hair.

### Is Shark’s 45-day dock larger than Dyson’s 30-day bin?

They are different systems and test conditions. Shark’s 2 L base stores transferred debris; Dyson compresses debris inside the 0.35-gallon onboard bin. Household debris determines the actual interval.

### Which has better filtration?

Shark publishes 99.97% at 0.3 microns or larger; Dyson publishes 99.99% down to 0.1 microns. Because methods and disposal differ, consider whole-system sealing and emptying exposure.

### Does Dyson V16 wash floors?

Only the separate V16 Piston Animal Submarine package includes the Submarine 2.0 wet roller. The standard dry V16 in this comparison does not.
