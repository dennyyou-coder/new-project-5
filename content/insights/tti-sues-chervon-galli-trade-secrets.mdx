On February 5, 2026, Shad Hansen received a text message.

Hansen was president of sales at R&B Sales & Marketing, the [TTI](/brands/tti) company that runs its North American store and field sales team. The message came from Joseph Galli, TTI's former chief executive, who had retired almost two years earlier. Galli wanted to talk about something "Private and Confidential."

They spoke the next day. According to TTI's complaint, Galli relayed details that Hansen had shared only two days before, with just three of his direct reports.

In TTI's telling, that call is how an eight-month "covert channel" came to light.

Nine months later, on November 9, TTI North America, [Milwaukee](/brands/milwaukee) Electric Tool and R&B sued Chervon North America, Chervon Holdings and Galli himself in the US District Court for the Northern District of Illinois. By then Galli was [Chervon](/brands/chervon)'s CEO. The complaint runs to 57 pages under case number 1:26-cv-13942, and the plaintiffs demand a jury trial. On November 12, Chervon's Hong Kong shares fell more than 9% intraday.

What follows retells the complaint in chronological order. Everything here is the plaintiffs' allegation, and the court has not ruled on any of it. I could not find a public response from Chervon or Galli. My own analysis will follow in a separate article.

## Two agreements

The story starts in May 2024.

That month, Galli retired from TTI. He joined in 2006, became chief executive around 2008 and held the role for roughly sixteen years. The complaint says that during that time he had access to TTI's most sensitive strategic, financial, product, sales and personnel information, knew which employees held confidential information, and knew who had signed restrictive covenants and what those covenants said.

He left with two agreements.

The first was a service agreement signed in January 2008. In it, Galli acknowledged that he would obtain confidential information about TTI's business, operations, customers and suppliers that "can be expected to be of value to competitors." He agreed to keep it secret during and after his employment, and never to use it to induce TTI employees, customers or suppliers to leave. The agreement also barred him, for two years after leaving, from soliciting or hiring TTI senior executives and employees with access to confidential information.

The second was a retirement agreement dated on or about May 16, 2024. Galli confirmed that he had returned all company property and confidential information and kept no copies. Going by the service agreement, the two-year non-solicitation period ran to around May 20, 2026.

The complaint states that TTI is not suing for breach of either agreement in this case. It cites them to show that Galli understood how sensitive and valuable the information was. It also says TTI was still making payments to Galli under the retirement agreement while the communications described below took place.

## The alleged covert channel

After Galli left, two senior sales executives followed.

Matthew DeFeo, TTI's group president of sales, had joined in 2006 and left in April 2025. Thomas Kentouris, senior vice president of sales for the southern division, joined in 2012 and left in August 2025.

One stayed. Daniel Abbott, known as Dan in the messages, was R&B's senior vice president of sales for the western division. He oversaw about 600 employees across 15 states and territories and received TTI's most sensitive sales and financial information. Like Kentouris, he had reported to DeFeo.

R&B is what TTI employees call "Team TTI." The complaint alleges that from summer 2025, messages among these four men became Galli's window into TTI.

![Timeline from the complaint, from Galli leaving TTI in May 2024 to the lawsuit on November 9, 2026](/images/articles/tti-sues-chervon-galli-trade-secrets/02-timeline.webp "All events are as alleged in the complaint.")

On June 22, 2025, Galli asked Abbott for the addresses of the two best locations of a large TTI distributor near Menlo Park, California, so that he could visit them privately. He added:

> "Ideally no rep will be there when I confidentially visit."

Abbott replied, "Yes sir." The next day he sent the store numbers and addresses. The complaint says that which stores counted as the best was itself TTI's confidential judgment.

On August 16, in an exchange with Kentouris and DeFeo, Abbott wrote:

> "I'm ready to follow Joe anywhere, for as long as he wants."

On October 23, Galli told Abbott he planned "to become a world class 'AI driven' CEO" and that "AI will change everything – including the global power tool market as we know it." He later wrote, "Alaska will be a significant growth opportunity for Pro Tools." Abbott replied, "We will be ready!"

That autumn, the complaint says, TTI information began to flow out through Abbott. He sent Kentouris screenshots of three restricted Team TTI National Sales Update reports, dated October 7, October 23 and November 29, showing distributor sales by company, region and division, with prior-year comparisons and regional rankings. The complaint says that on October 29, after a confidential customer meeting that day, he sent detailed notes to Galli and DeFeo.

On January 1, 2026, Abbott wrote to Galli, DeFeo and Kentouris that he was excited for 2026. Galli replied, "Happy New Year Gentlemen! Our future is incredibly exciting." Kentouris sent Abbott a separate message with an image: "Let's go! 5 months!" Abbott emphasized the image. In another message to the three the same day, Galli wrote:

> "Gentlemen – get ready – in the next 5 years we will change and shock the world yet again."

On January 15, Milwaukee's chief financial officer presented at a restricted meeting for leaders at senior vice president level and above. The slides were marked "Information in this deck is CONFIDENTIAL" and contained Milwaukee's non-public 2025 global net sales, EBIT, gross margin, SG&A, capital expenditure, inventory and tariff impact. The complaint alleges that Abbott sent screenshots of them to Galli.

Galli replied, "Worst year in history." Then: "Dan – our future is exciting!" Abbott answered, "Can't wait!!!"

The messages continued. On January 22, Galli wrote about hiring new graduates together in the years ahead, and Abbott liked the message. On January 24, Galli shared "some history"; DeFeo replied, "Let's do it again!!!" Galli wrote, "We will. Only will be even better. And not just tools." Abbott responded, "I'm ready. Let's go!" Later that day Kentouris remarked to Abbott that Galli had mentioned not just power tools. Abbott replied that Galli was "going to come with something big. Has had two years to figure out the approach, no distractions."

On January 28, Galli wrote that "we will transform our next 'platform company' with an infusion of newly minted engineering/technology grads." DeFeo replied, "We are going to crush everyone!!!" On January 31, Galli shared an image showing TTI with the leading share of the 2024 global power tool market and wrote, "We will be changing all this in the years ahead." Abbott responded, "Interesting, they are going down."

![Six messages quoted in TTI's complaint, from June 2025 to January 2026](/images/articles/tti-sues-chervon-galli-trade-secrets/03-messages.webp "Messages recovered from Abbott's company devices, as quoted in the complaint.")

## February 2026

On February 4, Hansen shared something with three direct reports, one of them Abbott. On February 5, Galli texted. On February 6, the complaint says, Galli repeated details that Hansen had told only those three people.

According to the complaint, TTI investigated and on February 11 fired Abbott for sharing confidential information with Galli. The complaint says that when his company phone was returned, R&B found that he had remotely removed his iCloud storage and Apple ID, wiping its contents.

On March 10, R&B sued Abbott in the US District Court for the Central District of California (case 5:26-cv-01118), seeking an injunction in aid of arbitration. The parties stipulated to certain injunctive relief.

The complaint says forensic examiners later recovered messages from Abbott's company devices between June 2025 and February 2026, exchanged with Galli, DeFeo and Kentouris. The messages above come from those records.

## Seven weeks at Chervon

On September 10, Chervon named Galli group chief executive. Four days later, on September 14, DeFeo became Chervon's senior vice president of sales, training and recruiting, reporting directly to Galli. Chervon's announcement highlighted his experience with TTI's process for recruiting university graduates and developing them into store sales staff, account managers and leaders.

Over the next seven weeks, the complaint lists Chervon's appointments date by date.

On September 28, Trevor Bithrey, TTI Canada's vice president of end-user marketing, became Chervon's vice president of professional end-user marketing and research in Canada, reporting directly to Galli. The complaint links the appointment to Chervon forming a global end-user research and jobsite marketing team. The same day, David Reguidel, a national account manager at TTI Canada, resigned. The complaint says a forensic audit of his laptop found that three days before leaving he copied a large number of files to an external storage device, including national-account information, the Job Site Solutions Canadian strategy for 2026–2027, a JSS mega-site strategy, account purchase details, target account data and detailed financial data. Upon information and belief, it says, he has started working for Chervon.

On October 5, Chervon appointed Neil Harrison, formerly TTI Canada's national field sales manager, as vice president of sales and marketing and managing director for Canada; Claudio Chiappetta, who introduced Milwaukee cordless products in Canada, as vice president of retail sales for Canada; and Peter Carlson, a former Milwaukee national sales manager, as director of sales for strategic accounts. The fourth appointment that day was Abbott, fired by TTI eight months earlier, as director of sales for the western division, reporting to DeFeo.

On October 12, Kentouris joined as vice president of field sales, also reporting to DeFeo; Chervon credited him and his teams with recruiting more than 2,000 graduates into TTI. The same day, Randall Durkan, TTI's senior vice president of supply chain, became vice president of supply chain for North America, reporting to Galli, and Charlie Harte, previously a sales director at a large TTI distributor in Canada and the US, became director of field sales and recruiting for Canada.

On October 13, Galli announced a Global End User Research Group reporting directly to him.

On October 19, Devin Murray, who had reported to Kentouris at TTI, became director of sales for the northern division, again reporting to Kentouris. Krystle Adams, who ran TTI's national and campus recruiting and internship programs, became director of sales recruiting with a mandate to build Chervon's campus recruiting system and hire hundreds of graduates. The complaint lists her latest agreement with R&B as dated September 8, 2026.

On October 20, Stephen Sarnocinski, senior vice president of Milwaukee's Job Site Solutions programme, left TTI and the same day became Chervon's president of end-user research, sales and service for the global commercial landscaping outdoor power equipment market, reporting directly to Galli. The complaint lists his Milwaukee agreement as dated August 5, 2026. Around the same date, Thomas Scherer, vice president of human resources for Team TTI, left; Chervon later announced him as vice president of organizational development from around November 2; the complaint says he told TTI he would handle organizational design, operations and human resources. Emma Scarito, Team TTI's director of training development, left around October 21 to become Chervon's director of training.

The complaint adds that before leaving, Scarito and Chiappetta attended a confidential TTI meeting on its strategy for winning against Chervon's [EGO](/brands/ego-power-plus) brand.

On October 26 came another group. Joel Petersheim, Milwaukee's vice president of industrial channel sales, became vice president of end-user research and sales for Chervon's commercial outdoor power equipment business in the US, reporting directly to Galli; the complaint lists his agreement as dated October 14. Maggie Jones, who had managed about 140 store-coverage representatives, became director of national field sales operations. Parker Wesley Clary, who had reported to Kentouris, became director of field sales for the southern division. Jose Castellanos, a Milwaukee equipment specialist, left TTI and joined Chervon the same day.

On October 30, Chervon launched its "Advanced Momentum Program" under DeFeo to recruit and develop hundreds of college graduates for roles from store coverage and end-user marketing to engineering, supply chain and finance. Around November 2, Jody Grimsley, a former Milwaukee senior national account manager who had left TTI on July 1, started at Chervon in the same role.

![The 20 people named in the complaint, grouped by function, with their new Chervon roles](/images/articles/tti-sues-chervon-galli-trade-secrets/04-hires-by-function.webp "Grouping and roles as described in the complaint.")

Galli also spoke publicly about hiring. In a YouTube interview he said Chervon had "already hired 11 . . . superstar caliber people," would "build the best team in the world for power tools and outdoor power equipment," and would "unleash our campus recruiting program" to hire hundreds of graduates. The complaint says he texted the interview to TTI employees, implied in it that TTI employees had called him on a burner phone, and personally texted TTI employees to recruit them.

## The numbers and the filing

TTI's count stops on November 3. The complaint says that between September 10 and November 3, Chervon hired at least 30 former TTI personnel, many within days of leaving and some on the same day. Of 19 external hires Chervon announced between September 1 and November 3, 18 came from TTI.

The complaint lists each person's agreements with TTI: confidentiality, duty of loyalty and trade-secret obligations, and in many cases 12-month post-employment restrictions on similar work for a competitor and on soliciting customers and employees. Some of the agreements name Chervon explicitly as a competitor. The complaint also alleges that Chervon set up no screen to stop these employees from using or disclosing TTI information.

Six days later, on November 9, the complaint was filed in Chicago.

It brings eight counts: misappropriation under the federal Defend Trade Secrets Act; misappropriation under the Illinois Trade Secrets Act; tortious interference with contract, brought separately by R&B, Milwaukee and TTI North America; unfair competition; aiding and abetting breach of fiduciary duty; and unjust enrichment. TTI calls the conduct willful and malicious and seeks punitive damages.

The relief sought includes compensatory damages and lost profits; disgorgement of profits Chervon earned through TTI's trade secrets, confidential information and customer relationships; return of TTI property; an injunction against using or disclosing TTI's confidential information; and preliminary and permanent injunctions barring Chervon from soliciting, recruiting or hiring TTI employees whose non-compete or non-solicitation obligations have not expired. The complaint does not state a dollar amount.

It also says Chervon's recruiting continues, TTI's investigation continues and the complaint may be amended to add claims and parties. TTI's contract claims against individual former employees are being pursued separately in the forums their agreements require.

As of this writing, the docket shows only four entries: the complaint, the civil cover sheet, an attorney appearance and a notification of affiliates. There is no injunction motion and no response from Chervon yet.
