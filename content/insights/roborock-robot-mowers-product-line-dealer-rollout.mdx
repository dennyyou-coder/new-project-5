**Roborock now sells robotic lawn mowers under the RockMow and RockNeo names. Its current US website lists the RockMow X1 LiDAR, RockMow X1 and RockNeo Q1, while the European site uses a broader RockMow Z1, RockMow S1 and RockNeo Q1 structure. The company has country product pages, online buying routes, distributor applications and regional after-sales contacts, but it does not publish a verified global robot-mower dealer count.**

That makes a simple answer such as “Roborock has dealers worldwide” incomplete. Model names, availability, accessories and warranty terms vary by market. A product listing also does not prove that a buyer has local installation, repair or parts support.

## Roborock Robot Mowers at a Glance

[Roborock introduced its first robotic lawn mowers at IFA 2025](https://newsroom.roborock.com/gl/news/roborock-expands-outdoor-portfolio-with-next-generation-rockmow-and-rockneo-series-of-lawnmowers-for-every-lawn-and-every-challenge). The European launch presented three products: RockMow Z1, RockMow S1 and RockNeo Q1.

The company then used CES 2026 to announce its [entry into the US lawn-mower market](https://newsroom.roborock.com/gl/news/ces-2026-roborock-releases-the-world-s-first-robotic-vacuum-with-wheel-leg-architecture-as-it-joins-hands-with-real-madrid-football-club-). The US lineup was described with different flagship names: RockMow X1 LiDAR, RockMow X1 and RockNeo Q1.

The current regional structure is:

| Region | Product names visible on the official site | Positioning |
|---|---|---|
| United States | RockMow X1 LiDAR, RockMow X1, RockNeo Q1 | LiDAR flagship, RTK/VSLAM AWD flagship and mainstream RTK/VSLAM model |
| Europe | RockMow Z1 LiDAR, RockMow Z1, RockMow S1, RockNeo Q1 | A four-level family with multiple area-rated variants |

This is a market snapshot, not a claim that every listed model is in stock in every country. Roborock uses national sites and model variants, and launch timing can change. Buyers should check the local product page and seller before treating a model as available.

## RockMow and RockNeo Are Regional Product Families

The public model names are not globally uniform.

Roborock’s [European warranty policy](https://garden.roborock.com/eu/policy/article/warranty-statement) identifies RockNeo Q105 and Q110, RockMow S108 and S115, RockMow Z115, Z130 and Z150, plus the RockMow Z120 LiDAR. Its EU Data Act notice also lists Q-, S-, Z- and X-series designations.

The [US mower site](https://garden.roborock.com/us) presents three customer-facing products: X1 LiDAR, X1 and Q1. The [US RockNeo Q1 page](https://garden.roborock.com/us/products/roborock-rockneo-q1) shows a starting price of $1,299 and identifies the listed variant as Q110H.

It is tempting to treat X1 as a simple US rename of Z1. The official pages do not provide enough evidence to map every X variant directly to a European Z model. Specifications, bundled accessories, software status and warranty coverage should be checked at model level.

For buyers and distributors, the safe process is:

1. record the full retail name and type designation;
2. confirm the country site on which the model appears;
3. save the current specification and warranty pages;
4. verify included positioning hardware and accessories;
5. confirm who handles warranty service in the sales territory.

## What Roborock Is Bringing from Indoor to Outdoor Robotics

Roborock describes its outdoor expansion as an application of its experience in environmental perception, motion control and automation. The product pages support that connection, but a lawn is not simply a larger living room.

### RockNeo Q1: mainstream wire-free positioning

The US Q1 combines full-band RTK with VSLAM under the Sentisphere name. Roborock also lists stereo-vision obstacle avoidance, automatic mapping and app-based zone management.

Its official daily-coverage claim is up to one-quarter acre in the United States, based on manufacturer testing during a 12-hour period that includes charging. The European page uses up to 1,000 square meters per day for the broader Q1 family. These are test claims, not guaranteed capacity for every lawn.

The Q1 is therefore the easiest entry point into the line, but it still depends on site conditions. Trees, walls, narrow passages, transitions and the placement of charging and RTK equipment can change performance.

### RockMow X1 and Z1: an AWD terrain platform

The [US RockMow X1](https://garden.roborock.com/us/products/roborock-rockmow-x1) adds all-wheel drive, active front-wheel steering, dynamic suspension and a floating cutting deck. Roborock states that it can handle slopes up to 80% and obstacles up to 3.1 inches. Those figures are based on internal manufacturer testing.

The platform matters because high slope capability is not only a motor-power question. Turning behavior, traction, chassis stability and cutting-deck movement determine whether a mower can cross difficult ground without damaging turf or losing cutting consistency.

The X1 uses full-band RTK and VSLAM. In Europe, the Z1 page describes the same Sentisphere combination for its non-LiDAR flagship family. Buyers should still compare the exact regional specification instead of assuming that the product bundles are identical.

### RockMow X1 LiDAR and Z1 LiDAR: a second positioning route

The [US RockMow X1 LiDAR](https://garden.roborock.com/us/products/roborock-rockmow-x1-lidar) combines 360-degree 3D LiDAR with VSLAM. Roborock positions it for dense or complex gardens where satellite-based positioning can be difficult.

This creates a clear two-route strategy. The standard X1/Z1 platform uses RTK plus vision, while the LiDAR version reduces dependence on an external satellite correction setup. The trade-off is a more complex sensing package that must remain protected, clean and supported.

See [RTK vs LiDAR Robot Lawn Mowers](/blog/rtk-vs-lidar-robot-lawn-mowers) for the navigation mechanisms. Roborock’s lineup is one current example of a manufacturer offering both routes rather than forcing every lawn into one positioning system.

## Several Promoted Functions Still Need Status Checks

Roborock’s product pages distinguish hardware specifications from functions scheduled for an over-the-air update.

Depending on the model and regional page, categorized obstacle behavior, automatic mapping, custom mowing patterns or parts of multi-zone management may carry an “upcoming OTA update” notice. A distributor should not copy a launch-page feature list into a quotation without checking the current firmware and country release.

PreciEdge needs the same care. Roborock describes a cutting module that reaches 1.2 inches, or 3 centimeters, from a boundary. The company also says this number is the structural hardware distance; actual cutting depends on the lawn edge and software logic. The module is sold separately on the current Q1 and X1 pages.

This distinction matters in dealer training. “Cuts to 1.2 inches from the boundary” is narrower and more defensible than “eliminates all manual edge trimming.”

## Roborock Is Actively Recruiting Distributors and Resellers

The [US distributor application](https://garden.roborock.com/us/become-a-distributor) accepts applications from distributors and resellers. It asks about company size, annual turnover, geographic coverage, product range and business type.

The mower-specific questions are more revealing. Roborock asks which robotic lawn-mower brands are established in the applicant’s market, how many units were sold there in the previous year, whether the applicant already distributes robot mowers and what after-sales service it can provide.

That is not a public dealer-count database. It is evidence of an active channel-screening process.

The form suggests that Roborock needs different partners for different roles:

| Partner capability | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Market access | Retail and ecommerce relationships create initial reach |
| Robot-mower experience | The partner understands seasonal demand, site constraints and customer education |
| Technical service | Warranty diagnosis and field support cannot be replaced by a product listing |
| Territory coverage | Sales, installation and repair capacity need a defined service radius |
| Category data | Local price bands, competitors and installed base affect the product mix |

This is the practical channel challenge for a robot-vacuum brand moving outdoors. Indoor robots are usually sold as appliances. Robot mowers add property conditions, outdoor positioning hardware, weather exposure, blades and a stronger seasonal service peak.

## Warranty Terms Show Why the Dealer Still Matters

Roborock’s [US mower warranty](https://garden.roborock.com/us/policy/article/warranty-statement) gives the RockNeo Q110H main unit two years of coverage. The listed RockMow X1 variants receive three years, while their batteries are covered for two years. Blades are consumables and are excluded.

The European policy follows a similar duration structure for the Q1, S1 and Z1 families but lists different regional type designations. The two policies should not be treated as interchangeable.

The policy tells customers who bought from a local dealer to contact that dealer with the serial number, warranty status in the app and proof of purchase. If remote diagnosis cannot resolve the issue, the product may have to go to a designated service center.

The same policy excludes rental and commercial use. A commercial operator should therefore request separate written terms rather than assume that a residential warranty applies to a fleet.

Warranty conditions vary by region. The European policy does not establish US coverage, and a US listing does not prove that a European service center will accept the product.

The dealer’s useful role is not limited to processing a sale. A capable partner should be able to explain:

- which model fits the site and positioning environment;
- what is included in the installation;
- which accessories and spare parts are stocked;
- who performs diagnosis and repair;
- how warranty claims move from seller to service center;
- what response time is realistic during the mowing season.

## How to Verify a Roborock Dealer or Distributor

Use a product-and-address test rather than a general brand claim.

### 1. Confirm the regional product

Match the seller’s exact model to the official country page. Record the complete type designation, included charging or positioning hardware, software status and warranty.

### 2. Check the seller relationship

Ask whether the seller is Roborock, an authorized distributor, an authorized dealer, a retailer or a marketplace reseller. These roles can have different return and warranty responsibilities.

### 3. Define installation

For RTK models, identify who checks satellite visibility and places the reference station. For LiDAR models, confirm how the site will be mapped and tested around trees, walls and narrow passages.

### 4. Verify the service route

Name the workshop or service center before purchase. Ask which parts are local, which failures can be diagnosed remotely and who authorizes warranty work.

### 5. Check commercial-use terms

Residential warranty language may exclude rental or commercial work. Fleet buyers need written coverage for duty cycle, turnaround time, replacement machines and parts.

### 6. Save dated evidence

Product pages, OTA notices, seller authorization and warranty terms can change. Save the relevant pages with the quotation and purchase record.

## What the Rollout Means for the Robot-Mower Market

Roborock is entering outdoor robotics with a wider technical ladder than its three 2025 launch names first suggested. It now shows RTK/VSLAM, LiDAR/VSLAM and AWD terrain platforms across regional product families.

The product architecture is only half of the market entry. The US site currently combines a narrow visible online buying route with an open distributor-recruitment program. That points to a channel still being built rather than a mature network that can be measured with one global number.

For established garden-equipment dealers, Roborock brings a recognized smart-cleaning name and a software-led product story. For Roborock, those dealers bring territory knowledge, site support and repair capacity that a consumer-electronics channel does not automatically provide.

The strongest distributors will connect both sides: digital demand generation and local service delivery.
