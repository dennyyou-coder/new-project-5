This week, the last booths at WCB were confirmed: 127 booths, 127 exhibiting companies. The show opens in one week.

In chronological order, the next episode of this series should cover 2024. I want to insert this one first, as a record of where the first edition stands before it opens. The 2024 story will continue after the show.

The [2026 WCB International Cleaning Appliance Expo](/wcb-expo) takes place from 18 to 20 November at the Suzhou Shishan Convention Center. At our Shenzhen exhibition in March, every booth was 6 m². This time, booths come in four sizes: 9 m², 18 m², 36 m² and 72 m². Converted to 9 m² standard booths, that is 227 booths on about 8,000 m² of exhibition space.

![WCB 2026 in numbers: 127 exhibitors, 227 standard booths and about 8,000 square metres](/images/articles/building-worlds-no-1-cleaning-show-from-scratch-episode-07/02-booths-and-space.webp "Every booth was confirmed a week before opening. The 30,000 m² target for 2027 is my own plan.")

At first, I gave WCB a three-edition plan. The first edition would lay the foundation, the second would expand, and the third would mature. In three years, it would become the world’s No.1 cleaning show.

Then I changed my mind. If the goal is world No.1, why not build the first edition to that standard?

Whether a show can lead its industry depends above all on its exhibitors. If the world’s most competitive cleaning companies are there, the show has the foundation to become No.1. So I changed how I sold the show, and contacted every leading brand I could think of.

![Featured brands and the full supply chain at WCB 2026](/images/articles/building-worlds-no-1-cleaning-show-from-scratch-episode-07/03-full-supply-chain.webp "Six leading brands, more than ten finished-product factories and dozens of component suppliers.")

In the end, [ECOVACS](/brands/ecovacs), [Roborock](/brands/roborock), [Midea](/brands/midea) cleaning, Segway-Ninebot’s [Navimow](/brands/segway-navimow), [WYBOT](/brands/wybot) and [BISSELL](/brands/bissell) confirmed. The talks went more smoothly than I expected. I explained to each company’s senior leaders what WCB is trying to do. Most of them quickly offered their support and assigned a team to work with me. Without these brands, WCB would only be an ordinary trade show.

Beyond the brands, more than ten finished-product factories are exhibiting, covering vacuum cleaners, robot vacuums, floor washers, window-cleaning robots, robotic mowers and pool cleaners. They are joined by dozens of component suppliers: motors, pumps, batteries, sensors, bearings, brushes, seals and moulds. Brands, finished-product makers, contract manufacturers and component suppliers are all there. The cleaning-appliance supply chain is essentially complete.

Until now, seeing the leading brands’ new products in one place meant travelling to IFA in Berlin, CES in Las Vegas or AWE in Shanghai, at a high cost in travel and time. This time, they are in Suzhou.

The leading brands also changed the size of the show. When sales opened in July, I planned two halls, about 12,000 m² and close to 200 companies. As sales went on, almost every leading brand chose Hall A. If Hall B opened on its own, it would probably not draw enough visitors. What exhibitors fear most is paying for a booth, building a stand and then watching no one walk past. In the end, I merged Hall B into Hall A. The show is smaller, but every booth gets traffic.

The venue changed once too. I first approached the Suzhou International Expo Centre in the Suzhou Industrial Park, but it had no suitable dates, so the show moved to the Shishan Convention Center in Suzhou New District. The stand-building contractor, recommended by a friend, has taken on a great deal of on-site and back-end work over the past few months.

The brands had to be signed one by one. Most component suppliers came back on their own. More than 90% had attended one of my earlier forums, or one of the two supply-chain exhibitions last November and this March. This time, I posted one notice in an industry group, and many companies confirmed straight away. Most of the other exhibitors found me through my videos, articles and live streams.

Many friends put it plainly: “Denny, I’m here because of you. I trust you, so I’m supporting you.”

I know these companies are exhibiting mainly because they trust me personally, not because WCB is already an established show. Part of that trust comes from the results of earlier events. Some companies won orders. Some reached large customers they could not reach before, and those conversations are still going.

The other part comes from further back. I started writing in 2017, nearly ten years ago. In those years I wrote a great many articles, made a lot of friends and helped many people along the way. None of it brought a breakthrough for my own business, and I never treated it as an investment. But when I began organising forums and exhibitions, the friends I had made stepped forward one after another to help. The seeds planted over ten years came back all at once. It was the first time I really felt that those years of effort had been worth it.

That trust also saved me a great deal of time. I have no business-development team and no sales team to follow up with each company. Because returning exhibitors came back on their own, I had time to work on the show itself.

But I also overestimated myself this year. I thought one person could handle everything. In reality, I can only have serious conversations with two or three companies a day, and that alone takes most of my time. To bring in more companies, I need a team. As of today, I am still the only person working on WCB full time; everything else relies on partners.

There were stretches when sales stalled. Progress was slow, other problems arrived at the same time, and the pressure was heavy. I did not know whether more companies would join, or whether the whole effort would be worth anything. Fortunately, whenever something went wrong, I usually found a solution by the next day.

Some friends advised me: “Don’t set such a big goal. Start with a small, focused supply-chain show. That’s valuable too.”

I did not take that advice. A small show that only serves the supply chain struggles to attract brands and buyers, and over time it shrinks. Even if a world No.1 goal falls short, the show it produces is still worth having.

This taught me that to get something done, you first need strong conviction. If you are certain it must happen and will happen, people can feel that certainty, and many will lend a hand. If your attitude is “I’ll do it if I can, and drop it if I can’t,” others will not commit either. No one wants to help someone who does not believe in themselves.

This week, every forum speaker has been confirmed, and the procurement matchmaking session is ready.

![The three-day programme at WCB 2026](/images/articles/building-worlds-no-1-cleaning-show-from-scratch-episode-07/04-three-day-programme.webp "The exhibition is open on all three days and admission is free for visitors. The organiser’s latest on-site notice applies.")

Beyond the booths, I have put a lot of work into four programmes.

The first is the forums. There are two sessions on 18 November, morning and afternoon, with eight speakers from different fields: securities analysts, analysts from a professional data company, and AI experts on how companies can put AI to work. On the morning of 18 November, I will give a keynote of my own: The Road to a Trillion, Together. Both forums are free for visitors.

The second is procurement matchmaking. After an earlier event, an exhibitor complained to me: “Denny, I know you invited a lot of customers, but none of them connected with me.” Customers came, but exhibitors never met them. I had to fix that. So this year, I set aside the morning of 19 November for a dedicated matchmaking session, attended only by exhibitors and professional buyers. Purchasing managers from about ten companies, including Roborock, Narwal, Kärcher and STIHL, will meet exhibitors one by one. However the rest of the show goes, every exhibitor will at least have a chance to talk face to face with these ten large companies.

The third is the product launches. There is one session on the afternoon of 19 November and another on the morning of 20 November, each with five or six exhibitors presenting new products and technology. A company with a good product can present it directly to the audience, and interested visitors will come to them. After the show, the launches will also be published on our WeChat account, giving exhibitors another channel to reach the market.

The fourth is the networking dinner. With so many people in Suzhou, everyone wants more chances to talk, whether with customers or with peers. So on the evening of 18 November, I have arranged dinner across dozens of tables for exhibitors and guests. The meal itself is not the point. On the show floor, many conversations last only a few minutes. In the evening, people can sit down and talk properly: you introduce me to a customer, I introduce you to one, and several companies may find a way to work together. More conversations mean more business.

![Visitors and exhibitors at the WCB exhibition in Shenzhen, March 2026](/images/articles/building-worlds-no-1-cleaning-show-from-scratch-episode-07/05-shenzhen-expo-march-2026.webp "Product and supplier discussions at the Shenzhen exhibition in March 2026.")

I gave this year’s show a theme: The Road to a Trillion, Together.

The theme matters a great deal to me. I have said publicly that the cleaning industry can reach a trillion yuan. Having said it, I need to answer several questions. Where will a trillion come from? How does the industry get there? And when it does, what will each person in the industry gain?

![The road to a trillion-yuan cleaning industry: WCB scenario estimate](/images/articles/building-worlds-no-1-cleaning-show-from-scratch-episode-07/06-road-to-a-trillion.webp "WCB scenario estimate, not third-party statistics or a forecast. The company-size hopes are my own view.")

By WCB’s estimate, the market for finished cleaning appliances, mainly household products, is worth less than CNY 300 billion today. With new categories such as commercial cleaning, pool robots and robotic mowers, plus robotics and global expansion, brand retail sales of finished products could reach CNY 1 trillion within ten years. Behind that would be about CNY 250 billion of contract manufacturing and about CNY 185 billion of core components. This is our scenario estimate, not third-party statistics or a forecast, but it reflects my view of where the industry is heading.

A trillion is not just an industry total. It means something specific for every kind of company in the chain. In the next 10 to 20 years, I hope to see a new cleaning brand reach CNY 100 billion in sales, a contract manufacturer reach CNY 10 billion, and component makers reach several billion. That is what a trillion is worth to everyone in this industry.

A trillion-yuan industry deserves a show of its own.

Where a trillion will come from, and how far each kind of company can go, is what I will set out in full at the forum on the morning of 18 November. You are welcome to come and listen, and to discuss it with me in person.

For this edition, my expectations have largely been met already. Thousands of professional visitors and exhibitors will be in one place, listening to forums, seeing new products, meeting new partners and doing some real business. That is the most important value of the first edition.

This edition lays the foundation for the next. For the next one, I will build a team, work with the government and bring in more overseas industry alliances and associations, so that together we can make WCB the industry’s true No.1. In 2027, I plan to expand the show to 30,000 m², three times this year’s size.

I want WCB to become the cleaning industry’s real annual festival. Every November, people from the industry around the world come to Suzhou to look for new business. That is why I am doing WCB.

So after the show, my focus will turn overseas. Some overseas buyers have already registered for this edition, but not many yet. Over the next few months, I will put much more effort into overseas markets, so that more people abroad know WCB and talk about it, and many more overseas visitors come to Suzhou next year.

I have written articles for ten years, and I make videos and live streams too. But I am more and more certain that the show is the hardest and most important of all these things. Articles and videos can be copied, and with AI, copying only gets faster. An in-person show needs brands, factories, suppliers, buyers and visitors to turn up in the same place, and it needs trust built over years. Once it works, it is very hard for anyone else to copy. That is why I am willing to bet the next ten years on WCB.

The road to a trillion, together. See you in Suzhou next week.
