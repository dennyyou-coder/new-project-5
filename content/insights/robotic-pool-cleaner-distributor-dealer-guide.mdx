**A good robotic pool cleaner distributorship is not the brand with the largest retail discount. It is the program that leaves positive contribution after landed cost, sales support, inventory aging, returns, warranty labor, spare parts and seasonal markdowns—while giving the distributor verifiable compliance files and a workable service route.**

Start with one region, three or four purposeful SKUs and a written pilot scorecard. Expand only after the first selling season proves sell-through, claim turnaround and parts availability.

## Choose the Distribution Model

“Distributor” can describe several different businesses:

- **master distributor:** imports, warehouses and supplies dealers;
- **regional distributor:** holds inventory and develops a territory;
- **pool dealer:** sells, demonstrates and supports end users;
- **installer/service dealer:** combines product sales with onsite diagnosis;
- **e-commerce reseller:** sells nationally but may outsource service;
- **drop-ship partner:** markets products while the brand owns inventory and fulfillment;
- **private-label importer:** becomes the manufacturer or responsible economic operator in several legal systems.

The obligations and economics change with the model. A drop-ship reseller avoids much inventory but has less control over delivery and returns. A master distributor earns on dealer supply but funds containers, warehouses, credit and compliance. A private-label importer gains pricing control and also inherits much more product-liability and documentation risk.

Write the model in one sentence before asking for a price list.

## Define the Customer

Product selection begins with the buyer and the pool, not the supplier presentation.

Useful segments include:

- entry residential above-ground pools;
- standard in-ground family pools;
- large or freeform premium pools;
- pools with tanning ledges and steps;
- tree-heavy properties needing surface skimming;
- saltwater pools;
- professional pool-service companies;
- hospitality and commercial operators.

Commercial use needs special attention. Many consumer warranties exclude rental, industrial or commercial operation. Do not promise hotel or service-route coverage based on a household warranty sheet.

Build a territory profile with pool count, construction type, season length, typical debris, installer density, electricity standards and customer price bands.

## Select Brands with a Channel Scorecard

A strong consumer brand can still be a weak distributor partner. Score each candidate on evidence.

| Area | Questions |
|---|---|
| Demand | Branded search, dealer leads, reviews, local awareness, repeat sales |
| Product | Pool fit, cleaning zones, battery, filters, app, retrieval, differentiation |
| Channel | Territory, dealer levels, lead allocation, online conflicts, price discipline |
| Economics | Net buy price, freight, rebates, payment terms, demo support, markdown fund |
| Operations | Forecast accuracy, lead time, minimum order, local inventory, fill rate |
| Service | Claim approval, diagnostic tools, parts, labor credit, replacement stock |
| Compliance | Model-specific reports, labels, responsible party, traceability, recall plan |
| Longevity | Firmware policy, app continuity, battery supply, successor compatibility |

Weight the score for the business. A service dealer may give parts and diagnostics 25% of the total; an online reseller may weight fulfillment, content and return process more heavily.

Do not accept a score supported only by sales slides. Link every important answer to a contract clause, certificate, stock report or pilot result.

## Build a Rational SKU Portfolio

Too many similar units create dead inventory and confused sales teams. A practical opening ladder is:

1. **entry:** floor-only or basic floor/wall/waterline cleaner;
2. **core:** the model that fits the majority of local pools;
3. **feature:** shallow-platform, fine-filter or surface-skimming model;
4. **premium:** advanced navigation, dock or low-maintenance system.

Add only accessories that solve known jobs:

- standard debris basket;
- fine filter;
- roller or active brush;
- track or wheel kit;
- power supply or charging dock;
- retrieval hook and caddy;
- replacement battery where authorized.

Record which parts are shared. Common filters and chargers reduce stock depth and service mistakes. A wide range with no shared service parts can consume more working capital than its revenue suggests.

## Calculate Contribution Margin

Gross margin based on the invoice is incomplete.

Use:

`net sales = selling price - discounts - refunds - sales tax collected`

`landed product cost = buy price + origin charges + freight + insurance + duty + brokerage + inbound handling`

`variable channel cost = card/marketplace fee + commission + pick-pack + delivery + installation/demo`

`risk cost = return reserve + warranty labor + warranty freight + parts reserve + markdown reserve + bad debt`

`contribution = net sales - landed product cost - variable channel cost - risk cost`

`contribution margin = contribution / net sales`

Illustrative scenario—replace every number with local quotations and history:

| Input | Scenario A |
|---|---:|
| Net sales | $1,000 |
| Landed product cost | $590 |
| Selling and fulfillment cost | $105 |
| Return/warranty/parts reserve | $90 |
| Seasonal markdown reserve | $40 |
| Contribution | $175 |
| Contribution margin | 17.5% |

The example is not an industry benchmark. Its purpose is to show why a nominal 41% buy-to-sell spread can become a much smaller contribution.

Run at least three cases:

- base season;
- weak sell-through with markdowns;
- high claim/return season.

The deal should survive the downside case without requiring the next dealer order to fund old warranty claims.

## Manage Inventory and Seasonality

Pool equipment is seasonal in many markets. Ordering late loses the peak; ordering too early ties up cash and ages batteries.

Track:

- weeks of supply by SKU;
- open purchase orders;
- confirmed factory and ocean lead time;
- local sales velocity;
- dealer backorders;
- battery manufacture date;
- firmware generation;
- weeks until the season ends;
- aged-stock markdown exposure.

Use an open-to-buy plan, not one annual guess. Separate display, service replacement and sellable inventory. A demo unit should never silently return to new-stock status.

Negotiate:

- order cancellation windows;
- rolling forecasts;
- end-of-life notification;
- successor transition;
- price protection;
- stock rotation or buyback;
- freight responsibility for shortages and damage.

The supplier’s minimum order quantity is not a demand forecast.

## Returns, Warranty and Failure Data

Ask for definitions before asking for a “failure rate.”

- DOA: failed at receipt or first use;
- customer return: can include fit, expectation or setup;
- no-fault-found: returned unit passes inspection;
- warranty claim: accepted under the written terms;
- field failure: technical defect after use;
- cosmetic damage: may be freight or handling;
- app issue: may be account, Wi-Fi, firmware or hardware.

A single percentage without numerator, denominator, period, model, geography and failure definition is not useful.

Create a return authorization path:

1. capture serial number, proof of purchase and pool/use conditions;
2. check setup, filter and charging issues;
3. collect photos, video and app/firmware information;
4. decide part, repair, replacement or rejection;
5. define inbound and outbound freight;
6. quarantine unsafe or water-damaged batteries;
7. record root cause and resolution days.

In the United States, written consumer warranties must meet applicable Magnuson-Moss and FTC disclosure requirements, including pre-sale availability. State rules and implied warranties also matter. Decide whether the brand, distributor or dealer is the warrantor and make that visible before sale.

## Parts and Consumables

The best time to buy service parts is with the machines.

Request:

- exploded parts list and revision control;
- part numbers by model and serial range;
- recommended opening stock;
- service prices and dealer discount;
- parts lead time and fill-rate target;
- minimum supply period after model end;
- warranty labor allowance;
- instructions and safety procedures;
- battery return and disposal route.

Measure:

`parts fill rate = order lines supplied complete / total parts order lines`

`claim cycle time = customer report to usable product`

`first-time fix = claims resolved without a second part or shipment / closed claims`

A high first-time fix rate can be more valuable than a low part price.

Wear parts should be available to consumers without forcing a full machine exchange. Filters and brushes also provide recurring revenue, but only if the SKU is stable and the retailer avoids confusing similar generations.

## Decide the Repair Capability

Choose one service level explicitly:

- **triage only:** dealer documents the case and ships the unit;
- **module swap:** trained dealer replaces approved baskets, tracks, motors, power supplies or boards;
- **depot repair:** regional center opens and reseals units;
- **replacement only:** defective units are exchanged within agreed conditions.

Opening an IP-rated pool robot without the correct process can compromise seals and electrical safety. Module repair requires training, torque specifications, seal replacement, leak or pressure testing and traceable parts.

The service agreement should cover:

- authorization to repair;
- training and tools;
- diagnostic software access;
- water-ingress classification;
- battery isolation;
- labor reimbursement;
- failed-part return;
- replacement ownership;
- turnaround target;
- data privacy for app-linked devices.

Never build the customer promise around a verbal “we usually replace it.”

## Compliance and Product Safety

Compliance is a model-specific file, not a logo collage.

For the United States, review the actual configuration for applicable electrical safety certification and FCC equipment authorization. OSHA’s NRTL program recognizes laboratories within defined scopes; a registered certification mark should correspond to the product and appropriate standard. Wi-Fi and Bluetooth devices must meet FCC authorization rules before import or marketing.

For the European Union, determine all applicable legislation, conformity assessment, technical documentation, EU Declaration of Conformity, CE marking, language, traceability and responsible economic operator. GPSR adds product-safety processes, accident and complaint handling, consumer recall communication and economic-operator duties. WEEE, batteries, radio equipment and other rules may also apply.

Cordless robots contain lithium-ion batteries. Transport may fall under UN3481 when batteries are contained in or packed with equipment. Packaging, short-circuit protection, labels, documents, state of charge and carrier acceptance depend on battery and route. Use current PHMSA, IATA, carrier and destination rules.

Collect at minimum:

- model list and photos of rating labels;
- test reports and certificates with model coverage;
- critical-component list;
- FCC ID or authorization evidence where applicable;
- EU DoC and responsible-person details;
- battery UN38.3 test summary and transport data;
- SDS where relevant;
- packaging and drop-test evidence;
- serial/batch traceability;
- recall and incident process;
- product-liability insurance certificate.

Have a qualified compliance professional review the target market. This guide is commercial due diligence, not legal advice.

## Recall Readiness

CPSC guidance says manufacturers, importers, distributors and retailers can have reporting and recall responsibilities. Selling recalled products is prohibited in the United States.

Before launch, make sure you can:

- identify every unit by serial or batch;
- identify which dealer or customer received it;
- stop sales across warehouse, dealers and marketplaces;
- send direct customer notices;
- quarantine returns;
- offer the approved remedy;
- report progress;
- reconcile destroyed, repaired, refunded and replacement units.

In the EU, GPSR also requires traceability and effective consumer remedies for recalls. A spreadsheet created after an incident is not a recall system.

## Supplier Due Diligence

Visit or audit the manufacturing and quality system when volume justifies it. Verify:

- legal entity and bank beneficiary;
- factory versus trading-company role;
- design and software ownership;
- incoming inspection;
- battery-cell traceability;
- leak, pressure, aging and final tests;
- change-control process;
- firmware release and rollback process;
- complaint CAPA;
- production capacity by month;
- existing territory conflicts;
- insurance and claims history.

Ask for anonymized claim data by model and month. Review the top failure modes and the corrective action, not only the average rate.

## Run a Pilot Order

A controlled pilot should cover real sales and service, not only samples.

Example pilot:

- one region;
- two or three dealers;
- three core SKUs;
- one demo per dealer;
- service-parts pack;
- eight to twelve weeks of active selling;
- weekly sell-through and issue review.

Define pass/fail targets before the order. Useful measures include:

- sell-through by SKU;
- return reasons;
- accepted warranty claims;
- claim cycle time;
- parts fill rate;
- training completion;
- demo-to-sale conversion;
- customer rating;
- price violations;
- inventory age.

Do not scale because the pilot inventory sold to dealers. Scale when dealers sell to end users and service the installed base.

## Negotiate the Channel Agreement

The agreement should cover:

- exact legal entities and territory;
- exclusive or non-exclusive status;
- minimum purchases and performance cure;
- channels and named accounts;
- online marketplace rules;
- price policy within local law;
- payment, credit and currency;
- forecast and allocation;
- lead ownership;
- marketing funds and claims;
- trademark and content use;
- warranty responsibility and labor;
- parts, tools and training;
- product changes and end-of-life notice;
- data and app responsibilities;
- insurance and indemnity;
- compliance documents and audit rights;
- recall cost allocation;
- termination, stock buyback and customer support.

Exclusivity without supply protection, lead allocation and exit stock terms can trap the distributor.

## Distributor Decision Scorecard

Score each area from 1 to 5 and multiply by the chosen weight.

| Area | Example weight |
|---|---:|
| Product-market fit | 20% |
| Contribution economics | 20% |

## FAQ

### What margin should a robotic pool cleaner distributor target?

There is no reliable universal public benchmark. Build a local contribution model including landed cost, selling fees, returns, warranty, parts, markdowns and finance. Test both base and downside seasons.

### How many models should a new dealer stock?

Usually three or four clearly differentiated roles are easier to sell and service than a broad overlapping range. Add SKUs only when they solve a distinct local pool problem.

### Should a distributor repair robots?

Only at the authorized level supported by training, parts, seal procedures, testing and labor reimbursement. Otherwise use triage, depot repair or replacement.

### What documents should be checked before importing?

Model-specific safety and radio evidence, labels, declarations, battery transport documents, traceability, manuals, insurance and recall procedures for the destination market.

### Is an exclusive territory automatically valuable?

No. It needs supply commitments, lead rules, online-channel boundaries, performance cures, service support and a fair exit-stock mechanism.

### What proves a successful pilot?

End-user sell-through, acceptable return reasons, fast claims, available parts and controlled inventory—not merely shipment from the supplier to dealers.
