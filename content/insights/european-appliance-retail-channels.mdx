A European consumer buying a vacuum cleaner might visit MediaMarkt or Carrefour, order from Amazon, or pick up a Silvercrest appliance during a Lidl promotion.

These channels sell similar products through different retail models. Specialist electrical stores offer a broad choice of brands and models. Supermarkets put appliances alongside everyday household shopping. Discounters sell private-label products through promotional programs, while online businesses widen the selection further.

For Chinese appliance companies, opportunities are spread across all of them. Looking only at specialist chains misses supermarkets and discounters. Concentrating exclusively on Amazon overlooks local retailers that have served their markets for decades.

This map organizes the main channels into five operating models. A company can run stores, an online shop and a third-party marketplace at the same time; placement reflects its principal retail characteristics. Banners within the same corporate group are discussed together. This is a channel reference, not a sales ranking.

## 1. Specialist Appliance and Consumer Electronics Retailers

### MediaMarktSaturn: Three Banners Across Multiple Markets

MediaMarktSaturn is one of Europe's largest appliance and consumer electronics retail groups. Its banners include MediaMarkt, Saturn and Italy's MediaWorld. Together, they operate more than a thousand stores across multiple European markets.

The assortment runs from refrigerators and washing machines to kitchen appliances, vacuum cleaners, televisions, computers and phones. Consumers can compare brands in one store. For manufacturers, that means competing directly on the same retail floor.

![Appliances and electronics on the retail floor at MediaMarkt Tech Village Cologne](/images/articles/european-appliance-retail-channels/02-store-floor.webp)

*MediaMarkt Tech Village Cologne. Official press photograph: MediaMarktSaturn / CECONOMY, August 2026. The image illustrates a specific store, not every location in the network.*

The group also operates online shops and third-party marketplace services. Physical stores provide display and sales space, while websites offer a wider selection. For vacuum cleaners, wet floor cleaners and robot vacuums, stores let shoppers see size, handling and cleaning assemblies in person. The [group's Cologne store announcement](https://www.ceconomy-mms.com/en/article/die-nachste-generation-des-technik-erlebnisses) describes how brand displays, advice and online services fit together.

MediaMarkt, Saturn and MediaWorld should not be counted as three independent groups. Assortments and operating arrangements can nevertheless differ by country.

### Fnac Darty: A Multinational Group That Retains Local Brands

Fnac Darty includes Fnac and Darty in France, Unieuro in Italy and Vanden Borre in Belgium.

Darty's traditional strengths lie in appliances and after-sales service. Refrigerators, washing machines, kitchen appliances and floorcare are important categories; delivery, installation and repair extend the customer relationship beyond the initial purchase. Fnac has traditionally focused more on cultural products, digital devices and consumer electronics.

Unieuro and Vanden Borre bring established positions in Italy and Belgium respectively. Retaining those banners preserves the familiarity consumers have with their local retailers. Fnac Darty is consequently better understood as a collection of locally established retail networks than as French stores replicated abroad. [Fnac Darty group profile](https://www.fnacdarty.com/en/group/)

### Currys: The UK and the Nordics

Currys is a major specialist electrical retailer in the UK and Ireland, selling major appliances, small appliances, floorcare and digital products. Its Nordic operations use different names.

**Elkjøp** in Norway, **Elgiganten** in Sweden and Denmark, and **Gigantti** in Finland all belong to Currys' Nordic business. Consumers encounter separate local banners, but the corporate connection is close. [Currys brand portfolio](https://www.currysplc.com/our-brands/)

For a manufacturer targeting the UK and Northern Europe, Currys is therefore more than a British account. Its networks can be studied at group level before country-specific category and purchasing arrangements are considered.

Greece's Kotsovolos used to belong to Currys. PPC acquired it in 2024, so it should no longer be included in the Currys network. [PPC acquisition announcement](https://www.ppcgroup.com/en/investor-relations/announcements/stock-news/stock-news-2024/ppc-completion-of-the-acquisition-of-kotsovolos/)

### United.b: Full-Service Electrical Retail and Discount Stores

United.b's portfolio includes **Boulanger and Electro Dépôt** in France, **Krëfel** in Belgium and **Hifi International** in Luxembourg.

Boulanger sells major appliances, kitchen products, floorcare and multimedia equipment, alongside delivery, installation and repair services. It also has private labels such as Essentielb and Listo, combining outside brands with products developed for its own assortment.

Electro Dépôt has a stronger discount positioning. Labels including Valberg and High One cover different appliance categories. Although it shares a group with Boulanger, its assortment and price proposition differ.

Krëfel and Hifi International extend the network into Belgium and Luxembourg. Together, these businesses illustrate two routes through European retail: using different banners to serve different markets, and participating in product development through private labels. [Boulanger and its group](https://www.boulanger.com/info/presentation-boulanger)

### POWER: One Brand Across the Nordics

POWER operates appliance and consumer electronics businesses in Norway, Sweden, Denmark and Finland. Its range includes major and small appliances, computers and phones.

Unlike Currys' use of several Nordic banners, POWER uses the same brand across these markets, combining stores with online sales. POWER and Currys' Nordic operations belong near the top of a specialist-retail research list for the region. [POWER company information](https://www.power.no/kundeservice/om-power/personvernerklaering/)

Beyond those two networks, Sweden's Elon, NetOnNet and Finland's Verkkokauppa.com have their own customer bases. Two large groups do not represent the entire Nordic market.

### Worten: An Important Route into Portugal

Worten belongs to Sonae and is one of Portugal's principal appliance and electronics retailers. It sells major and small appliances, computers and phones, operates online and service businesses, and also has operations in Spain. [Worten company profile](https://www.worten.pt/sobre-worten)

Its relevance is a reminder that retail strength in Europe is often country-specific. A company need not have the highest recognition across the continent to be an essential account in a target market.

Portugal also requires attention to **Radio Popular**, a specialist in major appliances, small appliances and consumer electronics. Together with Worten, Fnac and others, it forms an important part of the local channel landscape.

### Poland: Media Expert and RTV Euro AGD

Poland's specialist market extends well beyond MediaMarkt.

**Media Expert** describes a network of more than 600 stores selling appliances, electronics and digital products. Despite its name, it should not be confused with either the international Expert alliance or MediaMarkt. [Media Expert store network](https://sklepy.mediaexpert.pl/)

**RTV Euro AGD** also has a broad physical network and online business. **Neonet**, which joined the x-kom group in 2024, continues to operate in appliance and electronics retail. [RTV Euro AGD stores](https://www.euro.com.pl/sklepy.bhtml), [Neonet profile](https://www.neonet.pl/o-nas)

These domestic retailers reach cities throughout Poland. Their visibility in Chinese industry reporting is not a reliable measure of their local importance.

### Central and Eastern Europe: DATART, NAY and Altex

**DATART** in Czechia and **NAY** in Slovakia are important specialist electrical networks whose businesses came together in 2024. Their corporate relationship should be understood together, while their consumer-facing banners are distinguished by market. [Group annual report](https://datartgroup.cz/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Vyrocni_zprava_2024.pdf)

Romania's **Altex and Media Galaxy** belong to the same operating business, covering appliances and electronics. Media Galaxy uses a large-store format and should not be double-counted as a separate group. **Flanco** is another domestic network, currently emphasizing a discount positioning. [Altex profile](https://altex.ro/despre-noi/pag/), [Flanco](https://www.flanco.ro/)

In Bulgaria, **Technopolis and Technomarket** operate separate local electronics and appliance networks. Both belong on a channel list that goes beyond Western Europe's multinational groups.

### Other Specialist Networks to Include

Switzerland's **Interdiscount and Fust** deserve separate attention. Fust belongs to Coop and extends from electrical products into kitchens, bathrooms and installation services. It had 132 stores at the end of 2025. [Fust company information](https://www.fust.ch/footer/ueber-uns)

Ireland's **DID Electrical** is a domestic specialist. In Greece, **Kotsovolos, Public and Plaisio** each have established businesses. Public absorbed the local MediaMarkt operation, with the affected stores converted to Public + home. [Public announcement](https://publicgroup.gr/mediamarkt-public-plus-home/)

The Baltic markets include the **Euronics** network and Lithuania's **Topo Centras**. Slovenia and nearby markets require attention to **Big Bang**. In Turkey, **Teknosa, MediaMarkt and Vatan** should be included. Teknosa was established within the Sabancı group and operates physical retail, e-commerce and a marketplace. [Teknosa profile](https://yatirimci.teknosa.com/en-US/teknosa-at-a-glance)

These companies share a local consumer base as a characteristic, not a common owner. Smaller national markets are no reason to omit them from a European channel map.

## 2. Specialist Retail Alliances

### Euronics: A Network Beyond Corporate Ownership

Euronics brings together retail organizations from different countries while members retain their operating independence. Some stores use the Euronics banner; other members retain their own retail brands.

Its international membership list includes Boulanger in France, Krëfel in Belgium, Elon in Sweden, Interdiscount in Switzerland, Teknosa in Turkey and Sinersis in Spain and Portugal. [Euronics member directory](https://www.euronics.com/regions/members)

A retailer can therefore have two identities at once. Boulanger belongs to United.b and participates in Euronics. One is a corporate ownership relationship; the other is commercial cooperation.

Spain's **Sinersis** merits a closer look. Its banners include **Tien21, Milar and Euronics**. Consumers see different store names, while purchasing and marketing cooperation connects the businesses behind them. [Sinersis banners](https://www.sinersis.es/nuestras-marcas/)

### Expert and ElectronicPartner

**Expert** has retail cooperation networks in Germany, Italy and other European markets, selling appliances and consumer electronics. Local operators, their customer relationships and their service capabilities are central to the model.

**ElectronicPartner** is another important specialist cooperation network, particularly in Germany. **EP:** is associated with local specialist electrical stores, while **MEDIMAX** uses an electrical superstore format. [Expert](https://www.expert.org/), [ElectronicPartner](https://www.electronicpartner.com/)

These organizations allow dispersed local retailers to work together while preserving local businesses. A market map limited to large company-owned chains would miss this part of European appliance retail.

Italy's **Trony**, Austria's **RED ZAC** and Sweden's **Elon** also reflect cooperation among local operators, but they are separate organizations. Trony developed from the GRE purchasing organization; RED ZAC brings together independent Austrian specialists; Elon combines locally run member businesses with group support.

## 3. Hypermarkets, Discounters and Membership Warehouses

### Carrefour: Appliances Within the Hypermarket

Carrefour is more than a food supermarket. In markets such as France, its hypermarkets and online business sell major appliances, small appliances and floorcare.

The French website includes washing machines, refrigerators, dishwashers, coffee machines, air fryers, vacuum cleaners and robot vacuums. Outside brands appear alongside Carrefour Home products. Appliances are part of its household business, rather than merely an occasional promotional addition. [Carrefour France appliances](https://www.carrefour.fr/r/electromenager)

The difference from an electrical specialist is the range of reasons consumers have to enter the store. Families shopping for food and everyday supplies may encounter a small appliance on the same trip. That regular traffic can matter for products with a clear price and an easily understood function.

Assortments differ by country and format. A French hypermarket's appliance range cannot be applied automatically to a neighborhood supermarket elsewhere.

### Auchan and E.Leclerc: Appliances in French Mass Retail

**Auchan** also sells appliances. Its French business includes washing machines, refrigerators and dishwashers, alongside kitchen appliances and vacuum cleaners. Its relevance comes from combining hypermarkets, online sales and household shopping. [Auchan France major appliances](https://www.auchan.fr/electromenager-cuisine/gros-electromenager/ca-8100)

**E.Leclerc** belongs on the same market map. Its stores and website sell major and small appliances, supported by promotional activity and after-sales services. A list limited to Darty, Boulanger and Fnac would leave out an important part of where French consumers shop. [E.Leclerc appliances](https://www.e.leclerc/cat/french-days-electromenager?page=1)

The UK's **Tesco** also sells electrical products, including small appliances such as kettles, while its website includes third-party listings. For general retailers, the relevant measure is the local appliance business, not the total number of grocery stores. [Tesco kettle assortment](https://www.tesco.com/shop/en-GB/search?query=+kettles)

### Lidl: The Retailer's Own Appliance Brand

Lidl's appliance model differs from the broad, continuously displayed brand ranges of an electrical specialist. Private labels and non-food promotions are important entry points for understanding it.

**Silvercrest** is Lidl's kitchen and household brand, covering products such as kitchen appliances, vacuum cleaners and steam-cleaning equipment. In 2026, Lidl announced a new international brand presentation and a partnership with Andre Agassi. It is actively building a product identity consumers can recognize. [Lidl's Silvercrest announcement](https://corporate.lidl.fr/espace-presse/communiques-de-presse/silvercrest-x-andre-agassi)

![Silvercrest's official 2026 campaign featuring Andre Agassi and the brand's household-appliance identity](/images/articles/european-appliance-retail-channels/04-silvercrest.webp)

*Official Silvercrest / Lidl campaign material, March 2026. Advertising imagery illustrating the retailer's brand-building activity, not an independent product test.*

Lidl also operates non-food e-commerce in some markets, so the entire appliance business should not be reduced to a weekly promotion. Stores and online assortments work alongside each other, with products and supply cycles varying by market.

For contract manufacturers, the attraction is a retailer organizing its own branded assortment. Appliance brands face a retailer that can be both a sales channel and a product-brand owner.

### Aldi: Non-Food Products Sold in Promotional Batches

Aldi's non-food promotions offer another model. In the UK's **Specialbuys** program, products arrive on a regular schedule across kitchen, electrical, cleaning and other household categories, while stocks last. An appliance enters a specific promotion rather than necessarily joining a permanent, comprehensive model range. [Aldi Specialbuys](https://www.aldi.co.uk/specialbuys?pp=1)

The purchase may be a coffee machine, mixer or cleaning appliance featured that week. For suppliers, concentrated delivery and the promotional calendar consequently carry more weight.

Aldi's purchasing organizations, brands and selling arrangements differ by country. The UK program should not be treated as a universal operating rule.

### Costco: Appliances in a Membership Warehouse

Costco's European warehouse markets include the UK, Spain, France, Sweden and Iceland. Appliances form part of its membership-warehouse assortment, but its network is not spread uniformly across Europe. [Costco market disclosure](https://investor.costco.com/news/news-details/2026/Costco-Wholesale-Corporation-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2026-Operating-Results/default.aspx)

Costco UK sells refrigerators, washing machines, dishwashers, coffee machines, small kitchen appliances and floorcare. Its website assortment should not be equated with stock physically displayed in every warehouse. [Costco UK appliances](https://www.costco.co.uk/Appliances/c/cos_10)

Food retailers, discounters and membership warehouses all participate in household appliance spending. They organize their assortments differently from electrical specialists.

## 4. Department Stores and Home-Furnishing Retailers

### El Corte Inglés and John Lewis

**El Corte Inglés** is an important Spanish department-store retailer with operations in Portugal. Major and small appliances sit within a broader kitchen and home-shopping environment. [Appliance assortment](https://www.elcorteingles.es/electrodomesticos/)

The UK's **John Lewis** also sells kitchen appliances, floorcare and major appliances. Consumers can buy home furnishings and electrical products from a familiar department store without making a separate trip to an electrical specialist. [John Lewis electricals](https://www.johnlewis.com/electricals/c500001)

These retailers present appliances within a household setting, distinct from an electronics-led store. Brands entering them reach an existing home-shopping audience.

### BUT-Conforama and Harvey Norman

France's **BUT and Conforama** focus on furniture, furnishings and household equipment, including appliances. Their French businesses now form the BUT-Conforama group. [Group profile](https://groupe.butconforama.fr/le-groupe/)

Moving home, renovating or furnishing a new household often creates simultaneous demand for furniture and appliances. Refrigerators, washing machines and kitchen appliances can therefore reach customers through these home retailers.

Australia-founded **Harvey Norman** combines furniture, bedding, electrical products and computers in large stores, operating in Ireland and selected other European markets. Its format addresses a broad household purchasing occasion. [Harvey Norman Ireland](https://www.harveynorman.ie/pages/about-us)

### Argos: Another Form of General Merchandise Retail

**Argos** developed from catalogue retail into a digital-led business supported by stores and collection points. Appliances are one part of a broad assortment, but remain relevant to the UK's small-appliance market.

Its display model differs from a traditional department store, and its role differs from a purely third-party marketplace. General merchandise retail is the most useful context for understanding its position.

## 5. Online-Led Retailers and Marketplaces

### AO and Coolblue: Services Behind the Online Sale

**AO** is an important UK online electrical retailer with logistics and recycling capabilities. Major appliances are a significant part of the business, alongside small appliances and other electrical products. [AO company profile](https://www.ao-world.com/about-ao/)

**Coolblue** developed in the Netherlands and now operates in the Netherlands, Belgium and Germany. It reported €2.563 billion in revenue in 2025 and had 38 stores at year-end. Websites, stores and delivery and installation work together; store count alone does not capture its scale. [Coolblue 2025 review](https://aboutcoolblue.com/en/yearbook/a-year-in-review/)

![A customer receiving advice in a Coolblue store, showing the physical service side of an online-led retailer](/images/articles/european-appliance-retail-channels/05-coolblue.webp)

*Official Coolblue press photography supplied with its January 2026 store-expansion announcement. Representative store imagery; not labeled as a photograph of the then-unopened Cruquius store.*

Both businesses show why online appliance retail requires more than an order page. Delivery, installation and removal of an old refrigerator or washing machine affect whether a customer returns.

### Digitec Galaxus, Alza and eMAG

**Digitec and Galaxus** belong to the same company within the Migros group. Digitec's traditional emphasis is digital devices and electronics; Galaxus carries a broader assortment, including appliances and household products. They are important starting points for Switzerland's online retail market. [Corporate relationship](https://www.digitec.ch/en/guide/22)

**Alza** is an important Central European online retailer that expanded from electronics into appliances and other categories, supported by showrooms, collection and delivery networks. It belongs in research covering Czechia, Slovakia and Hungary, among other markets.

**eMAG** operates in Romania, Hungary and Bulgaria, combining retail with third-party marketplace services. It sells products itself and provides a route for outside merchants. [eMAG group profile](https://about.emag.ro/en/grup-emag-2/)

The Nordic list should also include **NetOnNet**, part of the Komplett group, and Finland's **Verkkokauppa.com**, an important online retailer with physical operations.

### Amazon and National Platforms

**Amazon** serves multiple European markets and is an important appliance channel. Germany's **OTTO**, France's **Cdiscount**, **bol** in the Netherlands and Belgium, and Poland's **Allegro** also have established positions in their respective markets.

Their direct-retail and marketplace structures differ. A product on a website may be sold by the retailer itself or a third-party merchant, and an online listing does not establish a physical-store placement.

For appliance companies, identifying the operating model before choosing a commercial approach is more useful than collecting retailer names. Specialist chains support continuing brand and product-line development; supermarkets and discounters provide other routes to volume; online-led retailers bring local audiences, logistics and service together. The right choice still depends on the product, price and target country.

For outdoor categories, see the separate [guide to robotic mower sales channels](/blog/robotic-mower-sales-channels): garden-equipment dealers and home-improvement stores play a different role from indoor-appliance retail.
