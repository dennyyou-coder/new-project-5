**For a business park, industrial estate or campus, choosing a robot mower is only half the decision. The other half is the service contract: who installs the robots, who maintains and monitors them, how fast they respond when a mower stops, and what you pay per season.** A strong contract turns a fleet of robots into a predictable grounds service. A weak one leaves facility staff chasing a stuck mower.

This guide lists what the contract should cover. For the equipment itself, see [commercial robotic lawn mowers](/blog/commercial-robotic-lawn-mowers); for the technical setup, see [robot lawn mower installation](/blog/robot-lawn-mower-installation).

## Who provides the service?

There are three common arrangements:

| Model | Who owns the robots | Who services them | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buy plus service plan | The site owner | Dealer or manufacturer service partner | Owners who want control and have a facilities team |
| Lease with managed support | Leasing company or dealer | Dealer | Spreading cost over 3–5 years |
| Mowing-as-a-service | Grounds contractor | Contractor, with its own robots and staff | Sites that want a fixed monthly price and no equipment risk |

Some grounds-care contractors and specialist robot-mowing firms offer the third model. If you are on the service side, see [how to start a robotic lawn mowing business](/blog/robotic-lawn-mowing-business).

## 1. Installation and site survey

The contract should state what the installer does before the first cut:

- **Site survey**: lawn areas, slopes, narrow passages, trees, car parks and public paths. Note where the public can reach the mowing area.
- **Navigation setup**: boundary wire, RTK base station or network RTK, LiDAR or vision mapping. Each needs different work; see [RTK vs LiDAR robot mowers](/blog/rtk-vs-lidar-robot-lawn-mowers).
- **Charging stations and power**: location, outdoor power supply, theft and vandalism protection.
- **Connectivity**: mobile data or site Wi-Fi coverage for every mowing zone, needed for monitoring.
- **Commissioning test**: an agreed test cut of every zone, with a signed acceptance before the service period starts.

## 2. Maintenance schedule

Ask for a written schedule rather than "regular maintenance":

| Task | Typical frequency | Who pays |
|---|---|---|
| Blade replacement | Every few weeks to two months in season, depending on grass and debris | Usually included |
| Cleaning chassis and sensors | Monthly in season | Included |
| Software and firmware updates | When released | Included |
| Battery health check | Each season | Included; replacement often extra |
| Winter service and storage | Once a year | Often extra |

Blades and batteries are the main consumables. Confirm which are included and the replacement price of each.

## 3. Response and repair times

This is where contracts differ most. Define:

- **Response time** after a fault alert, for example next working day.
- **Repair or replacement time**, and whether a **spare robot** is kept on site or at the dealer.
- **Who checks alerts**: the service provider's monitoring team or your staff.
- **Service credits** if mowing stops for longer than agreed.

## 4. Fleet monitoring and data

Commercial sites usually run several robots. The contract should give you:

- Access to the fleet dashboard or regular reports: area mowed, running hours, faults and downtime.
- Clear rules on who owns site maps and data, and where data is stored. Security teams increasingly ask this, and in the US new foreign-produced robots now need FCC approval; see the [FCC robot restrictions tracker](/topics/us-fcc-robot-restrictions).
- Geofencing and schedules that keep robots away from busy areas at set times.

## 5. Choosing robots for large sites

Capacity claims vary widely. Consumer-grade AWD robots now cover up to about 12,000 m² per map, such as the [Mammotion LUBA 4 12000F](/products/mammotion-luba-4-awd). Purpose-built commercial machines are much larger: PUDU's [GT5](/products/pudu-gt5) has a 60-inch rotary deck rated at about 2 acres per hour, and the [GT7](/products/pudu-gt7) is a triplex reel mower for golf and sports turf. For very large areas, compare one large machine with a fleet of smaller robots; see [robot lawn mowers for large lawns](/blog/robot-lawn-mowers-for-large-lawns) and [robot mowers for golf courses and sports fields](/blog/robot-lawn-mowers-golf-courses-sports-fields).

## 6. Price and how to compare offers

Compare offers on the same basis: **cost per square metre per mowing season**, including installation, maintenance, blades, batteries, monitoring and the cost of downtime. A low equipment price with expensive call-outs can cost more than an all-in monthly fee. Ask each provider for the same site plan and service levels before comparing.

## Contract checklist

1. Site survey and commissioning acceptance test included.
2. Navigation and connectivity set up for every zone.
3. Written maintenance schedule, with consumables listed.
4. Response time, repair time and spare-robot arrangement.
5. Fleet reporting, data ownership and security.
6. Insurance and liability for damage or injury.
7. Price per season, contract length and exit terms.
8. Named local service team, not just a national hotline. See [robot mower dealer networks](/blog/robot-mower-dealer-networks).
