**Shark and Roomba are the two robot vacuum names most American shoppers compare, but both companies have changed.** Shark is part of SharkNinja, a fast-growing listed appliance group. Roomba's maker iRobot went through restructuring and has been owned by its former contract manufacturer Picea since January 2026. Both still sell capable robots; compare matched models rather than brands.

## Which is better for mopping?

Roomba's 2026 Combo models use a roller mop that extends up to 45 mm to edges, with hot-water washing in the dock. Shark's flagship uses HyperSonic scrubbing and UV light to find stains. For hard-floor homes, the Roomba Combo approach is closer to the roller-mop trend; see the [robot vacuum buying guide](/blog/robot-vacuum-buying-guide).

## Which is better for pet hair?

Both have anti-tangle brushrolls. Shark's self-cleaning brushroll and bagless bases suit owners who want to avoid buying bags; Roomba's docks use bags. See [robot vacuums for pet hair](/blog/robot-vacuum-for-pet-hair-buying-guide).

## Does iRobot's ownership change matter?

iRobot is still based in Bedford, Massachusetts, and sells and supports Roomba under its own name. Picea, its new owner, was already its manufacturer. Check the warranty terms for your model and country as with any brand.
