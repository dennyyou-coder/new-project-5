**Operational Risks:**

There is a note regarding intellectual property (IP). If a third party uses IP that is shared with Xiaomi, Roborock would have no control over it.

> Some of Roborock's intellectual property is co-owned with Xiaomi. Under their cooperation agreement, either party may use the shared IP for its own products without notifying the other or sharing income, but neither may transfer or license it to a third party without the other's consent. The prospectus warns that these terms cannot guarantee that no third party will ever use the shared IP, which would hurt the business. (Prospectus, translated)

> Xiaomi is Roborock's customer, distribution channel and shareholder. Sales to Xiaomi made up 100.00%, 90.36% and 50.17% of main revenue in 2016, 2017 and 2018; if Xiaomi cut or stopped its robot vacuum purchases, results could suffer badly. Roborock also sells through Xiaomi's Youpin platform and Xiaomi's channels in Taiwan, so less promotion from Xiaomi would raise Roborock's marketing costs. Tianjin Jinmi, controlled by Xiaomi, holds 11.85% of Roborock, and Xiaomi's interests may at times conflict with those of other shareholders. (Prospectus, translated)

> All products are made by contract manufacturers; Roborock has no production base of its own. Its main manufacturer is Sunwoda, which received RMB 53.0 million, RMB 330.7 million and RMB 985.2 million of contract manufacturing fees in 2016, 2017 and 2018 – 99.68%, 100% and 98.80% of the total. A change in that relationship would hit capacity in the short term. (Prospectus, translated)

**Product Risks:**

> Revenue was RMB 183.1 million, RMB 1,118.8 million and RMB 3,051.3 million in 2016–2018, almost all from Mijia, Xiaowa and Roborock robot vacuums and their accessories. With a narrow product range, a big swing in robot vacuum demand – or a failure to keep up with what buyers want – would hurt the business. Net profit attributable to shareholders went from a loss of RMB 11.2 million to profits of RMB 67.0 million and RMB 307.6 million; the company warns it may not sustain that growth. (Prospectus, translated)

**Exchange Rate Risk:**

> The renminbi moved more against the US dollar during the period. Roborock's foreign-exchange result was RMB 5.73 million, −RMB 0.46 million and −RMB 1.67 million in 2016, 2017 and 2018. As exports grow, larger currency swings could affect results. (Prospectus, translated)

**Shareholding Structure:**

At listing, founder Chang Jing was the largest shareholder. Other holders included Shunwei Capital, Xiaomi's Tianjin Jinmi (11.85%), the employee platform Shitou Shidai, Ding Di, Banyan Consulting and QM27. Roborock Technology held 100% of Shenzhen Luoke, Roborock Innovation (with a Shanghai branch) and Roborock Hong Kong, which in turn owned Roborock's Hong Kong trading company and its US subsidiary. It also held minority stakes in two other companies.

**Detailed Explanation of IOT Platform:**

The planned data platform has four layers: data collection from devices, the mobile app and servers; a distribution layer (Kafka and similar message queues, log services); a processing platform for extraction, storage (MySQL, Redis/MongoDB, HDFS, Elasticsearch) and offline, real-time and multidimensional analysis, including A/B tests and machine learning; and a presentation layer for reports, alerts and third-party access. Its outputs are user-behaviour analysis, device monitoring, marketing support and smart services, with privacy, regional compliance and security audits built in.
