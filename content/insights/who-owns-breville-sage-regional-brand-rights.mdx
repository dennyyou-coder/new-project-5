**Breville Group Limited owns the global premium appliance business that uses Breville and Sage according to regional trademark rights.** In North America and many other markets, products are sold as Breville. In the United Kingdom and Europe, the group says it does not own or operate the Breville trademark, so it markets its premium designed products under Sage.

**Who manufactures Breville and Sage appliances?** Breville Group designs and develops the product range and works with external manufacturing partners to produce it. Its public reporting does not provide a complete model-to-factory map, so the exact production site, legal manufacturer and country of origin must be verified from the regional model label, compliance file and current supply documents.

Breville and Sage are therefore not simply two unrelated appliance companies. They are aligned regional consumer identities within Breville Group's business—but the legal seller, warranty company, model number and product specification can change by market.

## Who Owns Breville Group?

Breville Group Limited is an Australian public company listed on the ASX. Its roots go back to the Breville business founded in Sydney in 1932. The listed company reports the global appliance business and portfolio, including Breville, Sage, Baratza and LELIT in relevant markets.

Public-company ownership means shareholders own the parent rather than one outside appliance conglomerate. The group directs product development, brand strategy and regional operations through subsidiaries and partners.

## Why Is Breville Called Sage in the UK and Europe?

Breville Group's 2025 annual report gives a direct answer: the Breville trademark in the UK and Europe is not owned or operated by BRG, so the group markets and distributes its premium designed and developed kitchen products under Sage.

This is a trademark and market-rights boundary, not evidence that Sage products come from an unrelated engineering company. The group says Sage's identity and positioning are aligned with the global Breville brand.

The practical result is that a familiar espresso machine or countertop oven may carry Breville branding in the United States and Sage branding in Europe. Packaging, model suffixes, electrical specifications and service documents should follow the local mark.

## Are Breville and Sage Products Identical?

Do not assume exact identity solely from appearance. Regional versions may share core industrial design and technology while differing in:

- model reference and regulatory label;
- voltage, frequency and plug;
- accessories, programs or included consumables;
- certification and food-contact documentation;
- software or connected features; and
- warranty issuer, duration and service network.

A parts diagram or compatibility statement is stronger evidence than a retailer comparison. Cross-region substitution can create electrical, certification and warranty problems.

## Where Are Breville and Sage Appliances Made?

Breville Group describes a global product-development and supply model and works with external manufacturing and retail-channel partners. Its public materials reviewed for this guide do not provide a complete current map assigning every Breville or Sage model to a named factory.

That means broad claims such as “Breville makes everything in Australia” or “all Sage machines come from one Chinese factory” are not supported. The group is Australian, but corporate headquarters and factory location are different questions.

For a specific model or sourcing project, request:

1. the full regional model code;
2. the rating label and country of origin;
3. the legal manufacturer and production site;
4. current compliance and food-contact files;
5. the invoice seller, importer and warranty company; and
6. service-parts availability for the intended usage.

## What Products Does the Group Sell?

Coffee and espresso equipment is central to the premium business, including home espresso machines, grinders, drip coffee and accessories. The range also includes countertop ovens, air fryers, food processors, blenders, juicers, mixers, kettles and related kitchen appliances.

Breville Group also owns specialist coffee brands such as Baratza and LELIT. Shared group ownership does not automatically make their parts, dealer agreements or warranties interchangeable with Breville or Sage products.

## Who Handles Breville and Sage Warranties?

Breville's North American warranty defines Breville by jurisdiction as Breville USA or Breville Canada. Sage's reviewed European terms identify Sage Appliances GmbH for relevant transactions.

Those examples show why the logo alone is incomplete. A consumer warranty, retailer return policy and distributor service contract are separate obligations. Products imported across regions may not qualify for the destination market's official service.

For commercial users—cafes, offices or hospitality buyers—also check whether the warranty permits the intended duty cycle. A premium domestic espresso machine is not automatically covered for continuous commercial use.

## What Buyers and Distributors Should Verify

1. Confirm whether Breville or Sage is the authorized mark in the destination market.
2. Match the model suffix, voltage and included accessories to that market.
3. Identify the actual invoice seller, importer and warranty provider.
4. Request factory, origin and compliance evidence for the exact model.
5. Check whether use is domestic or commercial under the warranty.
6. Verify parts, cleaning supplies and qualified service availability.
7. Do not relabel products across territories without written trademark and distribution rights.

## Frequently Asked Questions

## Final Answer

Breville Group Limited owns the premium appliance business behind Breville and Sage. Breville is used in markets including North America, while Sage is the group's name for its premium products in the UK and Europe because the group does not own or operate the Breville trademark there.

The regional naming relationship is clear; the exact manufacturing and service relationship is model-specific. Verify factory, origin, local seller, importer and warranty terms before purchasing or distributing across markets.
