**Choose the Dreame X60 Ultra when low furniture and raised thresholds stop your current robot, and you prefer rotating mop pads. Choose the ECOVACS DEEBOT X12 OmniCyclone for mostly hard floors where dried food and footprints are the daily problem, and you want a roller mop with stain pre-treatment and a dock without dust bags.**

Both are 2026 flagships at almost the same US price, but they solve different problems. Dreame built X60 Ultra to reach more of the home. ECOVACS built X12 OmniCyclone to clean hard floors more thoroughly once it gets there.

## Mopping: Two Different Machines

This is the real choice, and it is not about which mop is "better" in general.

**X12 OmniCyclone** uses a 27 cm roller that is rinsed continuously by 32 pressurised water jets while it cleans, so the surface touching the floor keeps getting fresh water. Before mopping, FocusJet sprays targeted water on dried stains to soften them. The roller extends up to 2.58 cm towards edges. This design suits kitchens and dining areas where sticky marks and dried spills are the recurring problem. Spray pressure is not suction, and the floor still has to tolerate wet cleaning.

**X60 Ultra** uses two rotating thermal pads. Dreame describes 40°C warm-water mopping on the floor and dock washing at up to 100°C. Pads are removable, and the robot's mop handling around carpet depends on the mode chosen.

If your floors are mostly sealed hard surfaces with frequent spills, the roller-plus-pre-spray approach is the more targeted design. If rooms are mixed and access is the bigger issue, the pad design on a slimmer, better-climbing robot is the safer starting point.

## Reaching the Room: X60 Ultra Leads Clearly

The threshold ratings are far apart. Dreame lists 4.5 cm for a single threshold and 8.8 cm for a double-layer one. ECOVACS lists 2.4 cm single and 4 cm double for X12 OmniCyclone. Do not substitute one robot's higher double-step figure for the other's.

X60 Ultra is also documented at 79.5 mm tall with its sensor retracted (102.8 mm raised). We could not verify robot dimensions for the X12 reference SKU; package dimensions on retail listings are not robot dimensions. Ask for the local specification sheet if furniture clearance matters.

## Carpet

ECOVACS lifts the roller 15 mm and adds a protective cover when the robot meets carpet. That is not automatic removal, so test thick rugs and their edges. On X60 Ultra, check how carpet-first and vacuum-only modes behave in the app before relying on it around rugs.

## Docks: Bag or Bagless

Dreame's dock collects dust into a 3.2 L bag, with 4.2 L clean-water and 3.0 L used-water tanks. The dock measures 390 × 423 × 499 mm.

The OmniCyclone station uses a reusable 1.6 L bagless dust container. ECOVACS advertises a 48-day interval, which varies with how much debris your home produces. Bagless is not maintenance-free: the container needs emptying and its filter needs cleaning. ECOVACS also claims a 13% battery top-up in three minutes during mop-washing breaks.

ECOVACS's claim of up to 1,000 m² per task depends on automatic water refill and drain hardware. A tank-based X12 will not run that long without refills. Dreame sells a separate hookup kit for X60 Ultra.

## Price and Running Costs

ECOVACS announced X12 OmniCyclone on 14 April 2026 at USD 1,499 in the US and CAD 1,999.99 in Canada. Dreame's US store listed X60 Ultra at USD 1,499.99 on 6 September 2026. In other words, the entry prices are effectively equal.

Running costs differ in kind. X60 Ultra needs dust bags as well as pads, brushes and filters; Dreame's US manual suggests mop pads every one to three months. X12 avoids dust bags but still needs filters, brushes, replacement rollers and cleaning solution. Compare local pack prices over two years.

## Which One Should You Buy?

### Choose Dreame X60 Ultra when:

- thresholds above 2.4 cm or stepped transitions block your current robot;
- low furniture matters and you want a documented 7.95 cm body;
- your floors are a mix of hard floor and rugs;
- you are comfortable buying dust bags.

### Choose ECOVACS DEEBOT X12 OmniCyclone when:

- most floors are sealed hard surfaces;
- dried stains and sticky spills are the daily problem;
- you want edge mopping from an extending roller;
- you prefer a bagless dock and accept emptying and filter care.

## FAQ

### Which is better for kitchen stains?

X12 OmniCyclone is designed for this: FocusJet pre-sprays dried stains and the roller is rinsed continuously while it mops. Results still depend on the stain, the floor and the chosen mode.

### Which climbs higher thresholds?

X60 Ultra. Dreame lists 4.5 cm single and 8.8 cm double; ECOVACS lists 2.4 cm single and 4 cm double for X12.

### Is X12 OmniCyclone's dock really bagless?

Yes. It uses a reusable 1.6 L dust container instead of disposable bags. The container still needs emptying and its filter needs regular cleaning.

### Why does X60 Ultra claim more suction?

Dreame lists 35,000 Pa and ECOVACS 22,000 Pa with 22 L/s airflow. Both are manufacturer figures measured differently, so they do not show which cleans better in a real home.

### Were they the same price at launch?

Close to it. ECOVACS announced USD 1,499 in the US on 14 April 2026, and Dreame's US store listed X60 Ultra at USD 1,499.99 on 6 September 2026.
