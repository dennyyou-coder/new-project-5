The European Commission opened an anti-dumping investigation into robotic lawn mowers originating in China on November 19, 2025. The [official initiation notice](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/C/2025/6235/oj/eng) followed a complaint lodged on October 6 by Husqvarna Manufacturing CZ, alleging dumping and injury to European Union producers.

The product definition covers electric robotic mowers capable of cutting grass without direct human control. It includes machines supplied with or without associated equipment such as charging stations and navigation hardware. Both boundary-wire systems and wireless alternatives appear in the notice's description, so the investigation is not limited to one guidance technology.

The proceeding gives the Commission a framework to examine pricing, industry injury and the relationship between them. The notice sets out procedures for interested parties, including exporters, producers and importers, to provide information. It also explains how sampling and access to the investigation file will operate.

Opening the case is a procedural decision rather than a finding that the allegations have been proved. The November notice did not itself impose an anti-dumping duty or establish a final tariff rate. For mower companies, the immediate development was the start of a formal trade investigation covering the defined Chinese-origin products, with further decisions dependent on the evidence gathered.
