The UK Trade Remedies Authority opened an anti-dumping investigation into robot lawn mowers imported from China on November 18, 2026. The [TRA said](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/tra-investigates-imported-robot-lawn-mowers) the application came from [Husqvarna](/brands/husqvarna), based in Newton Aycliffe, County Durham, which alleges that dumped imports are injuring UK industry.

The case covers battery-powered autonomous mowers, including models guided by cameras or other sensors. Exporters, importers and other parties that want to take part must register through the Trade Remedies Service by December 3. The TRA has also opened a survey for businesses and consumers that use robot lawn mowers.

If the authority finds injurious dumping, it must also run an economic interest test, weighing the wider effect on UK buyers and businesses, before it recommends any duty. No finding or rate has been announced.

In a [statement](https://www.husqvarnagroup.com/sites/husqvarna/files/pr/202611181295-1.pdf), Husqvarna Group said it was confident in the merits of its case and would support the investigation. The UK proceeding is separate from the European Union's anti-dumping case on the same products, which [Husqvarna's Czech unit started](/news/eu-chinese-robot-mower-investigation-november-2025) in 2025. A decision on definitive EU measures is due by January 19, 2027.

Because the UK left the EU before that case began, any EU duty will not apply at UK ports. The new investigation means Chinese robotic mower makers now face anti-dumping cases in both the EU and the UK.
