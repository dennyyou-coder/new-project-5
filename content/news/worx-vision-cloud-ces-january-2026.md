Worx introduced its Landroid Vision Cloud robotic mowing platform at CES on January 6, 2026. The company combined stereo-camera perception with cloud-based RTK positioning, aiming to simplify installation by removing the need for boundary wire or a dedicated positioning antenna on the property.

The [launch announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/worx-unveils-landroid-vision-cloud-redefining-autonomous-lawn-care-with-ai-and-rtk-cloud-precision-302654336.html) describes stereo vision as a way to build a three-dimensional understanding of the surroundings. Visual simultaneous localization and mapping, or VSLAM, is intended to help the mower navigate where trees or other obstructions weaken satellite positioning. Onboard processing connects those navigation inputs with obstacle recognition and mowing decisions.

Worx also described a four-wheel-drive version rated for slopes up to 40 degrees, equivalent to an 84% gradient. That capability belongs to the specified variant and should not be applied to every mower in the range. The platform includes app-based control, with features and configuration varying between models.

An anti-theft package combines an alarm, location tracking and remote recovery support, offered as standard on selected models and optionally on others. The CES introduction therefore covered both a navigation architecture and a family of equipment choices. Garden size, slope capability and the features included with the selected model remain separate parts of the product specification.
