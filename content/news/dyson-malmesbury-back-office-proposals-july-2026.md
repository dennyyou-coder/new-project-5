[Dyson](/brands/dyson) proposed reducing about 170 back-office roles at its Malmesbury campus, the Gazette and Herald reported on July 21, 2026. The company said it was reviewing support functions while continuing to recruit for creative and engineering positions in the United Kingdom.

Dyson described the UK as its principal centre for creative and inventive work. The proposed staffing changes were intended to reflect that focus, according to the company's statement. The newspaper also cited an unnamed source who said IT roles were among those affected, although it did not provide a complete departmental breakdown.

For employees whose redundancies were ultimately confirmed, Dyson said it would provide outplacement support. The reported package included three months of career coaching, job-search guidance and help with applications and interviews. That wording makes the procedural stage clear: the July report concerned a proposal and support arrangements, rather than confirmation that every affected employee had already left.

Malmesbury remained a research, design and development site and the home of the Dyson Institute of Engineering and Technology. The campus continued combining engineering education with work on future products. The staffing proposal did not announce a campus closure or an end to Dyson's UK development activities.
