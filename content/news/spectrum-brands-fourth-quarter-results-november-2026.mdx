Spectrum Brands, whose Home & Personal Care business sells [Russell Hobbs](/brands/russell-hobbs), George Foreman, Black+Decker-branded appliances and Remington, reported fourth-quarter fiscal 2026 net sales of USD 713.2 million on November 12, down 2.8% from a year earlier. Organic sales, which exclude currency effects, fell 3.1%. The quarter ended September 30.

According to its [results release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000109177/000010917726000042/earningsreleaseexhibit991.htm), the biggest drop came in Home & Garden, where sales fell 9.3% after poor weather and high retailer stocks reduced reorders. Global Pet Care sales fell 1.5%.

Home & Personal Care sales fell 0.9% to USD 293.4 million, or 2.3% on an organic basis. Personal care was flat and home appliances were down by low single digits. In North America, the segment's sales fell by low double digits, which the company attributed to lost distribution in home appliances and stronger competition in personal care. Both categories faced more competition online. Retailers' orders ahead of a new company-wide software system lifted sales in the quarter.

Profit in the segment improved. Adjusted EBITDA for Home & Personal Care rose 58.6% to USD 24.9 million, with the margin up from 5.3% to 8.5%, helped by lower tariff rates and cost savings.

For the group, gross margin rose to 37.6% from 35.0% and adjusted EBITDA rose 10.3% to USD 69.9 million, but net income from continuing operations halved to USD 26.4 million because of a smaller tax benefit. Full-year net sales rose 1.5% to USD 2.85 billion; full-year adjusted EBITDA of USD 374.8 million included USD 57.4 million of net refunds of IEEPA tariffs, which the [US Supreme Court struck down](/news/supreme-court-ieepa-tariff-ruling-february-2026) in February. For fiscal 2027 the company expects flat to low-single-digit growth in sales and in adjusted EBITDA, excluding the refunds.

Chairman and chief executive David Maura said the company's plan for Home & Personal Care was unchanged. Its Empower Brands subsidiary, jointly owned with funds managed by Oaktree Capital, has offered USD 37 a share for Helen of Troy, owner of the OXO, Hydro Flask and Osprey brands; Helen of Troy's board [rejected the offer](/news/empower-brands-helen-of-troy-proposal-november-2026) on November 4. Maura said he was disappointed but remained optimistic.
