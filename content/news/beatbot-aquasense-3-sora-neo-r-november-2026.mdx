[Beatbot](/brands/beatbot) unveiled two cordless robotic pool cleaners on November 19, 2026: the AquaSense 3, a flagship for in-ground pools at a list price of USD 1,699, and the Sora Neo R, a USD 599 model built mainly for above-ground pools. The company showed both at Piscine Global Europe in Lyon and the Pool Spa Patio Expo in New Orleans, and said they launch in Europe and North America at the same time.

The AquaSense 3 uses a camera system Beatbot calls HybridSense AI Vision, which it says recognises more than 40 types of debris. The robot first goes after debris it can see, then cleans the edges and covers the floor in an S-shaped pattern, and finally rescans the pool and returns to spots it missed. Beatbot calls this sequence "Sense-to-Act" and describes the AquaSense 3 as the first pool cleaner to work this way; that is the company's own claim.

Other features include 2D mapping, a quick-clean mode that Beatbot says cuts cleaning time by up to 50% for lighter jobs, cleaning of shallow platforms from 18 cm of water, and up to five hours of floor cleaning. When it finishes, the robot returns near the point where it was put in the pool.

The Sora Neo R has two pumps with a combined flow of about 19 m³ an hour (5,000 gallons an hour), front roller brushes and tracks, and cleans the floor, walls and waterline. Beatbot says no other robot at its price has two pumps. When the job is done or the battery runs low, it rises to the surface, drains and parks at the edge, and it can hold clarifier or chlorine tablets that dissolve as it cleans. It weighs 7.2 kg and also works in simple flat-bottomed in-ground pools.

Beatbot did not give a shipping date. The two models extend its range at both ends: the AquaSense 3 sits next to the [AquaSense X](/news/beatbot-aquasense-x-ces-january-2026) it showed at CES in January, and the Sora Neo R takes it into the cheaper above-ground segment, where most of the volume is.
